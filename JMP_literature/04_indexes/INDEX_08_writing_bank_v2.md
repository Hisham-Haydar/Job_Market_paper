## 1. Latent Jobs As The Empirical Unit

The paper models labor supply as choice among latent jobs: feasible packages of
hours, wages, and job characteristics, plus non-work, rather than as choice over
a common hours grid. This is the core object of the Dagsvik-Jia and
Aaberge-Colombino RURO tradition [Dagsvik & Jia 2016, explicit; Dagsvik et al.
2014, background; Aaberge & Colombino 2013, explicit; Capeau & Decoster 2016,
explicit]. The standard fixed-grid model remains the benchmark and foil [Van
Soest 1995, explicit; Bargain et al. 2013, explicit], but it cannot carry the
household-specific opportunity density required by the JMP. Do not present the
latent-jobs literature as a welfare-family or decomposition source.

## 2. Opportunity Heterogeneity Versus Preference Heterogeneity

The central empirical claim is not that preferences are irrelevant; it is that
some observed labor-supply heterogeneity can be generated by heterogeneous
opportunities rather than tastes alone. The accepted literature supports the
separation of preferences from opportunity distributions in parametric
cross-sectional labor-supply models [Dagsvik & Jia 2016, explicit; Beffy et al.
2019, explicit; Capeau & Decoster 2016, explicit]. Bargain et al. motivate the
gap by noting that fixed-grid welfare models can absorb omitted demand-side
constraints into preferences [Bargain et al. 2013, explicit]. Do not overclaim
nonparametric identification or causal identification; the separation is
model-conditional and in the JMP is disciplined by synthetic recovery [JMP
project state v1, explicit].

## 3. Why Occupation Belongs In The Opportunity/Access Layer

In the JMP baseline, occupation enters as availability, not as taste and not as
an estimated wage return: `loc4` is an access shifter and `lindi`/industry is
reserved for robustness [JMP project state v1, explicit; JMP welfare spec v5,
explicit]. This choice is not directly inherited from the accepted RURO papers,
which mostly omit occupation [Dagsvik & Jia 2016, explicit absence; Capeau &
Decoster 2016, explicit absence; Beffy et al. 2019, explicit absence] or conflate
occupation/sector with utility and availability [Aaberge & Colombino 2013,
explicit warning]. Write this as a design discipline and robustness frontier, not
as a claim already established by the literature.

## 4. Why Wage Technology Is The Ability Channel

The ability channel is the wage-technology component of the opportunity density:
returns to education and experience plus residual productivity dispersion [JMP
project state v1, explicit; JMP welfare spec v5, explicit]. The closest
literature anchors are Dagsvik & Jia's wage opportunity component and individual
ability language [explicit] and Capeau & Decoster's lognormal wage-offer density
[explicit]. Fleurbaey-Maniquet's compensation-versus-own-skill axis supplies the
normative reason this channel is contested [Fleurbaey & Maniquet 2018 JEL,
explicit/derived-by-analogy]. Do not claim that existing papers identify the
JMP's full access/ability split; that burden remains with the JMP model and
recovery gate.

## 5. Why Money-Metric Welfare Must Respect Feasible Sets

The welfare object is an ex-ante money-metric equivalent income computed from
attained utility over the feasible set [JMP welfare spec v5, explicit; JMP
project state v1, explicit]. The money-metric and preference-respecting
foundation is supplied by the equivalent-income and Fleurbaey-Maniquet lineage
[Bargain et al. 2013, explicit; Decancq et al. 2015, explicit; Fleurbaey &
Maniquet 2018 JEL, explicit; Fleurbaey & Maniquet 2018 IJET, explicit], while
the nonlinear-in-income random-utility inversion apparatus comes from Dagsvik &
Karlstrom [explicit]. Do not attribute the ex-ante constrained-set object to the
JEL or IJET theory papers; the ex-ante set valuation is the JMP's empirical
construction.

## 6. Why W^1-W^6 Is A Responsibility-Sensitivity Surface

The six measures are best introduced as a responsibility-sensitivity surface:
Full Responsibility, one-sided compensation, and Full Compensation, organized by
Independence of pay/ability and Independence of access [JMP welfare spec v5,
explicit]. Published sources support the primitives behind the surface:
reference choice and compensation versus laissez-faire [Fleurbaey & Maniquet
2018 JEL, explicit], preference-respecting money metrics [Decancq et al. 2015,
explicit; Fleurbaey & Maniquet 2018 IJET, explicit], and empirical
equivalent-income welfare menus [Bargain et al. 2013, explicit]. Do not say
these sources contain W^1-W^6; the family is imported from the companion theory
paper and used empirically in the JMP.

## 7. Why Shapley Access/Ability/Preference Decomposition Is Appropriate

The decomposition problem is an allocation problem with interactions: equalizing
one channel changes the marginal value of equalizing another. Shapley-Shorrocks
is appropriate because it averages over orderings and yields an exhaustive,
order-independent allocation [Shorrocks 2013, explicit; Audoly et al. 2025,
explicit; Sastre & Trannoy 2002, explicit]. Shorrocks 1982 is the warning that
unaxiomatized factor decompositions can be arbitrary [explicit]. Do not write the
components as causal effects; they are model-conditional source contributions to
inequality in `Omega^k` [JMP welfare spec v5, explicit].

## 8. Why Microsimulation Is Supporting Infrastructure, Not The Paper's Identity

EUROMOD is the arithmetic tax-benefit engine that turns each job package into
disposable income; it is not the opportunity model, welfare theory, or
decomposition rule [Sutherland & Figari 2013, explicit; JMP project state v1,
explicit]. This infrastructure is standard in the labor-supply welfare
literature [Bargain et al. 2013, explicit; Capeau & Decoster 2016, explicit],
but the JMP's contribution is the consistent treatment of opportunities across
estimation, welfare, and decomposition. Do not let the paper read as a EUROMOD
methods paper or as a country-ranking application.

## 9. Why The Paper Is Not A Country-Ranking Exercise

The empirical baseline is France 2015-2017, but the object is source
decomposition of welfare inequality under heterogeneous feasible job sets, not a
country or beyond-GDP ranking [JMP project state v1, explicit]. The welfare
literature supplies country-comparative or policy-simulation comparators
[Bargain et al. 2013, background; Aaberge & Colombino 2013, background; Jacquet
et al. 2026, background], but the JMP's central object is an opportunity surface
over measures and channels [JMP welfare spec v5, explicit]. Do not write the
intro as if the contribution is France-specific ranking.

## 10. Why The Paper Is Not Merely Another Structural Labor-Supply Application

The paper uses structural labor supply, but its claim is not only behavioral
response estimation. It ties one opportunity density to three layers:
estimation, ex-ante money-metric welfare under feasible sets, and
Shapley-Shorrocks access/ability/preference decomposition [JMP project state v1,
explicit; JMP welfare spec v5, explicit]. The accepted literature supplies
pieces: latent jobs and identification [Dagsvik & Jia 2016, explicit; Capeau &
Decoster 2016, explicit; Beffy et al. 2019, explicit], money-metric welfare
[Bargain et al. 2013, explicit; Dagsvik & Karlstrom 2005, explicit], and
Shapley decomposition [Shorrocks 2013, explicit; Audoly et al. 2025, explicit].
The contribution is the integration and the responsibility-sensitive opportunity
surface; do not overstate any single source as already doing the full exercise.

## 11. Why Observed Labour-Supply Choices May Be Constrained By Job Offers

Observed hours-of-work are not the outcome of unconstrained choice over a common
hours grid; they are the joint product of individual preferences and a demand-
side job-offer structure. This claim has two layers. First, hours in a job offer
are often fixed by the employer [Bloemen 2008, explicit; *JoLE* 26(1):137–179],
so the choice set is not the full hours line but the set of packages the employer
is willing to offer. Second, the probability of receiving any offer at all —
involuntary non-participation — is governed by an offer-arrival mechanism
distinct from preferences [Bloemen 2000, explicit: Poisson offer count with
P(n=0)>0 — DP version, journal pages TBC; Bloemen 2008, explicit: arrival rate
λ governed by education, age, region]. Do not present this as a new claim unique
to the JMP; the job-offer-restriction tradition establishes it as a recognized
structural model class [Bloemen 2000, explicit; Bloemen 2008, explicit].

**Overclaim warning:** Do not use this paragraph to assert that Bloemen
supports the JMP's money-metric welfare, W^1–W^6, Shapley decomposition, or
proposal-corrected RURO likelihood. The Bloemen line contains none of these
objects. Its offers arrive stochastically (Poisson arrivals); the JMP's
feasible sets are deterministic. State the structural analogy and note the
framing difference.

## 12. Why Hours Packages Are Not Merely Free Continuous Choices

Even within a single job, the hours level is typically set by the employer —
the offer is a (wage, hours) bundle, not a wage rate over which the worker
chooses hours freely. Structural evidence for this is direct: the empirical
hours-offer distribution in Bloemen's Dutch job-search sample is sharply
concentrated at institutional standard hours (40h/week, probability ≈0.55–0.57),
far more concentrated than the distribution of desired hours reported by the same
workers [Bloemen 2008, explicit; *JoLE* 26(1):137–179]. Among employed workers,
≈68.6% are satisfied with their hours, but ≈25.3% report being overemployed
(want fewer hours) and only ≈6.1% underemployed [Bloemen 2008, explicit].
Desired participation is ≈8 percentage points above observed participation in
the Bloemen 2000 Dutch sample, quantifying the involuntary non-employment share
[Bloemen 2000, explicit — DP version, journal pages TBC]. These magnitudes are
not directly transportable to France 2015–2017 (different country, period, and
sample composition), but they establish that hours constraints are empirically
first-order.

**Overclaim warning:** Do not use specific Dutch magnitudes (40h offer
probability, 8pp participation gap) as numbers for the France JMP. Use them as
plausibility benchmarks only. Do not attribute a welfare interpretation to these
figures; they are behavioural diagnostics.

## 13. Why Constrained Offers Support Modelling Opportunity Separately From Preferences

The key consequence of job-offer constraints for the JMP is not just that
observed hours differ from desired hours, but that omitting the offer side
inflates estimated preference heterogeneity. Bloemen (2008) shows directly that
the base model — which fits observed hours without an external desired-hours pin
— assigns the largest random-preference variance and produces a nearly flat
reservation-wage profile; adding the external pin shrinks preference variance and
restores the expected reservation-wage shape [Bloemen 2008, explicit;
*JoLE* 26(1):137–179]. An identification warning from the earlier Bloemen (2000)
paper shows the stronger result: once the opportunity mechanism is made richly
covariate-dependent in a single cross-section, preference parameters cease to be
identified — the utility function becomes "flat and approximately uniform" [Bloemen
2000, explicit — DP version, journal pages TBC]. Together these results form the
canonical historical precedent for the JMP's synthetic-recovery discipline: the
access/preference split must be disciplined by a recovery gate, not just by in-
sample fit.

**Overclaim warning:** The "opportunity-omission inflates preference variance"
lesson from Bloemen 2008 operates via a missing hours-offer pin in a job-search
model; the JMP's mechanism is a missing opportunity density. Transport by
analogy, not identity. The identification-collapse result from Bloemen 2000
pertains to a Poisson arrival intensity made covariate-dependent, not directly
to the JMP's specification; cite as historical precedent. Do not say that Bloemen
proves the JMP's access/preference split is identified — the JMP's identification
rests on its own synthetic-recovery certification and the documented rejection of
the gender-split specification.

## 14. Why Wage Exogeneity Matters For The Ability Channel

The ability channel in the JMP's three-way decomposition is carried by the wage-
technology sub-block: returns to education and experience plus residual
productivity dispersion. A standard maintained assumption of discrete-choice
structural labor-supply models — including those in the RURO tradition — is that
wages are exogenous to (or independent of) preferences. Löffler et al. (2014)
provide the clearest available evidence that this assumption is consequential:
across 3,456 estimated discrete-choice labor-supply specifications, elasticity
estimates are largely insensitive to utility functional form and preference
heterogeneity but highly sensitive to the wage-imputation procedure and to the
wage-preference independence assumption. Relaxing independence (jointly estimating
wages and preferences with a multivariate-normal correlation structure) raises the
total elasticity from ~0.2 to ~0.6 for single females — a threefold change on the
same data [Löffler et al. 2014, explicit; IZA DP 8281 / SOEPpapers 675]. This
motivates treating wage-offer assumptions as a first-order robustness axis in the
JMP rather than a footnote.

**Overclaim warning:** Löffler et al. is not a RURO or latent-jobs paper; its
models do not implement an estimated multidimensional opportunity-offer density.
Apply the wage-exogeneity caution by analogy to the JMP's ability channel. The
~0.2→~0.6 result is for single females only (Germany/SOEP, 2007) and should not
be generalized to the France JMP setting. Do not cite Löffler et al. as support
for the JMP's decomposition design or welfare objects.

## 15. Why Cluster-Robust Inference Is Required For The Empirical Implementation

The JMP's decomposition confidence intervals and parameter standard errors will
be computed on EU-SILC/EUROMOD data, where households (`idorighh`) form the
natural cluster unit: multiple observations from the same household in the
pooled cross-section share unobserved correlated errors, making default or
heteroskedastic-robust standard errors too small and confidence intervals too
narrow. Cameron & Miller (2015) is the authoritative practitioner anchor: within-
cluster error correlation downward-biases default standard errors, with inflation
factors reaching 3–5× in applied examples [Cameron & Miller 2015, explicit;
*Journal of Human Resources* 50(2):317–372]. The appropriate response is the
cluster-robust variance estimator (CRVE sandwich) and — for the JMP's headline
inference — the pairs or wild cluster bootstrap. With ≈9,657 `idorighh` clusters,
the JMP is firmly in the many-clusters regime where the standard CRVE and pairs
bootstrap are well-behaved and the few-clusters corrections (wild bootstrap as a
necessity, CR2/CR3) are not required [Cameron & Miller 2015, explicit].

**Overclaim warning:** Cameron & Miller 2015 is an econometric inference survey;
it has no contact with structural labor-supply models, opportunity densities, or
welfare measurement. Do not cite it for anything beyond clustered-data inference.
"Two-way clustering" in Cameron & Miller refers to clustering along two error-
covariance dimensions (e.g. state and year); it is not the JMP's three-channel
(access/ability/preference) decomposition — the terms are unrelated. The Hersch
industry/occupation example in Cameron & Miller is illustrative of cluster-level
choice; it carries no implication for the JMP's `loc4` (occupation-access) or
`lindi` (industry, reserved for robustness) distinction.
