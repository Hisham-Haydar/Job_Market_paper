## 1. One-paragraph relevance to my JMP
This is a **boundary/contrast** paper. Bloemen estimates a *collective* household model in which the household optimum is split into individual egoistic preferences plus a **sharing rule** dividing non-labour income between husband and wife, recoverable up to an additive constant à la Chiappori/Donni. My JMP treats the couple as a single **unitary** decision unit with one household money-metric welfare object and does not attempt to recover individual shares; Bloemen is the canonical statement of exactly the intra-household identification problem I declare out of scope. It speaks to none of my three channels (preference / ability / access) directly — its decomposition is preferences vs sharing rule, not opportunities vs preferences — but it is the right citation for the limits of my unitary simplification and for why a credible intra-household extension would require panel data and a sharing-rule apparatus I do not have.

## 2. Data, setting, and model in brief
- **Country/years:** Netherlands, Socio-Economic Panel (SEP), 1990–2001.
- **Sample unit:** couples living together (married or unmarried), **without children**, husband aged 22–60, no self-employed; pooled 8,049 couple-year observations (5,558 married, 2,491 unmarried; 2,389 distinct households). <cite index="13-7,13-8,13-9">Couples without children are selected, the male is in the 22–60 range, self-employed households are excluded, and the pooled dataset contains 8,049 couple-years.</cite>
- **Model/method:** simultaneous system for hours, participation, and wages of both spouses; quadratic-in-wages, linear-in-non-labour-income labour supply (Hausman–Ruud underlying utility) allowing backward bending; estimated by **simulated maximum likelihood** (60 replications, smooth GHK-type simulator) with random effects exploiting the panel.
- **Feature I do not have:** a genuine **panel** (Bloemen exploits random effects across waves) and an estimated **sharing rule**. My France EU-SILC/EUROMOD base is a pooled repeated cross-section with ~20% two-wave overlap, no panel identification, and no sharing rule.

## 5. Opportunity mechanism
**N/A as an opportunity model — flag explicitly.** Bloemen has **no opportunity-density / feasible-job mechanism** in my sense. The choice set is the standard continuous hours line with a participation margin; "non-participation" is a **corner solution / reservation-wage** outcome of preferences and the sharing rule, not a constrained or unavailable feasible set. <cite index="25-2,25-3">The model does not incorporate demand-side conditions on employment, so it makes no distinction between non-participation and involuntary unemployment.</cite> In my access/ability vocabulary: there is no **access** channel (no modelled job/hours availability, no region/market primitive) and no **ability** channel as a wage *technology with returns inside the opportunity layer* — wages are modelled (a Mincer-type wage equation with education, age, year, with selection correction), but they sit in an econometric selection apparatus, not in an estimated availability density. The participation frontier is a property of tastes and the sharing rule, the opposite of my premise that feasibility differs across households.

## 6. Welfare object
**Largely N/A for my money-metric layer; do not conflate.** Bloemen computes **indirect utility** for each spouse and reports the *sign* of utility responses to wage and non-labour-income changes (sample percentages experiencing a utility increase), and analyses who gains as bargaining power shifts. <cite index="20-6,20-7">The indirect utility belonging to the specification is used to compute marginal effects of wage rates and non-labour income on utility, evaluated across observations and replications of the random effects.</cite> This is **not** a money-metric, preference-respecting equivalent income; there is **no reference object, no compensation–responsibility spectrum, and no equivalent-income root-solve**. Critically, its welfare is **individual-spouse** welfare mediated by the sharing rule — a fundamentally different object from my **single household-level** money metric. It is **ex post** (conditional on realised wages/shares), not the **ex-ante inclusive-value** object my paper requires. It sits **nowhere** on my $W^1$–$W^6$ map and carries **no** Ind-$y$/Ind-$A$ stance. The only honest relation: it illustrates the *intra-household welfare heterogeneity* my unitary object deliberately suppresses.

## 7. Inequality / decomposition content
**N/A for inequality; the "decomposition" is a different cut — flag explicitly.** Bloemen reports no inequality index and no inequality decomposition. He does perform a **behavioural decomposition**: total wage/income effects on labour supply are split into a **preference (structural labour supply) component** and a **sharing-rule component**, the latter recoverable up to an additive constant. <cite index="17-3,17-4">Using the reduced-form estimates, the parameters of the individual labour supply functions and the sharing rule are computed, so the total effects of wages and non-labour income on labour supply can be decomposed into effects from individual preferences and effects from the sharing rule.</cite> This is **preferences vs sharing rule**, not my **access / ability / preference** Shapley–Shorrocks split. It is **not reusable** for my three-way decomposition: there is no opportunity channel to attribute, the split rests on a within-household allocation primitive my unitary object excludes, and it is not an order-independent inequality decomposition. It would *not* extend to my three channels; it answers a different question (who controls resources within the couple) than mine (how much welfare inequality is opportunity-driven).

## 8. Identification and separation of preferences from opportunities
**Identification here separates preferences from the sharing rule, not preferences from opportunities — the distinction matters.** The Chiappori (1988)/Donni (2003) result lets the sharing rule be recovered up to an additive constant from cross-spouse derivatives of the labour-supply system, under **egoistic (or caring) preferences** and **no household public goods** (hence the childless-couple sample). <cite index="8-3,8-4">The possibility of recovering the sharing-rule parameters separately from the preference parameters stems from the assumption of egoistic preferences and from the wage fraction entering through the sharing rule.</cite> Two transport problems for my France pooled cross-section: (i) it identifies an **intra-household** object I do not target, and the additive constant is **not identified**, entangling with preference heterogeneity — <cite index="10-10,10-11">it is not possible to identify whether additive heterogeneity in the labour-supply function comes from heterogeneity in preferences for leisure or from heterogeneity in the level of the sharing rule.</cite> (ii) It leans on **cross-wage variation and continuous hours** with panel random effects; I have neither continuous-hours identification of a sharing rule nor a panel/external instrument. A coherence subtlety worth citing: with non-participation in a collective model, each partner's participation enters the other's, so the system can be **statistically incoherent**; Bloemen derives the sufficient condition $|s_m s_f|<1$. <cite index="11-9,11-10">Without further restrictions the system may generate multiple participation outcomes, and coherence is shown to hold for all combinations with the product of the cross-participation parameters below one in absolute value.</cite> This is a coherence issue **specific to two-decider collective participation**; my unitary couple does not incur it, which is one concrete benefit of the simplification.

## 9. Key results and magnitudes
- **Sharing rule by marital status.** Unmarried couples split non-labour income roughly fifty-fifty (coefficient ≈ 0.5); married couples assign close to all of it to the husband (coefficient ≈ 1). <cite index="19-7,19-8">For the unmarried the non-labour-income coefficient is around 0.5, indicating an even split, while for the married it is slightly larger than one, suggesting a substantial part is assigned to the husband.</cite> Restricting the sharing rule to be equal across marital status is **formally rejected**.
- **Backward bending.** Married men's labour supply is backward-bending over much of the wage range; women's is not. <cite index="16-5,16-6">Computing the husband's own-wage marginal effect, it is negative for 67.7% of married men versus 7.4% of unmarried men, indicating a backward-bending curve for married men.</cite>
- **Elasticities.** Female labour supply is more wage-responsive than male; magnitudes for women are in the usual empirical range. (Population: childless Dutch couples, 1990–2001.)

## 12. What I can cite this paper for
- The **canonical empirical collective model with non-participation** — to mark the intra-household allocation problem my **unitary** household object deliberately sets aside.
- The **statistical-coherence** issue in two-decider participation models ($|s_m s_f|<1$) — as a concrete cost a collective treatment incurs that my unitary couple avoids.
- The **preference-vs-sharing-rule non-identification of the additive constant** — supporting my claim that identifying *individual* well-beings inside a couple needs structure (egoistic preferences, panel, no public goods) I do not impose, justifying deferral of intra-household distribution.
- The **assumptions a collective treatment requires** (egoistic/caring preferences, no public goods, childless sample) — to motivate why a credible intra-household extension is a separate project, not a baseline tweak.
