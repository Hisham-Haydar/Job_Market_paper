## 1. One-paragraph relevance to my JMP
This is the survey that defines, in close to its modern form, the exact structural object my JMP builds on: the Random Utility–Random Opportunities (RURO) model, in which the choice probability is the systematic utility weighted by an **opportunity density** $p(w,h)$ over jobs, with $p$ factorised into a market-participation piece $p_1$, an hours density $g_1(h)$, and a wage density $g_2(w)$ (their Eq. 7). That factorisation is the literature antecedent of my $g$ and of the split between my **access** sub-block (hours / participation availability) and my **ability** sub-block (the wage technology) — though the paper itself does **not** use access/ability/preference language and does **not** perform any access/ability/preference decomposition. It is also a primary source for two of my estimation-layer primitives: the **sampling-of-alternatives re-weighting** (their Eq. 11, Ben-Akiva–Lerman) that underlies my mandatory $-\log\pi$ correction, and the **common-utility money-metric / equivalent-income** apparatus (their §5.5, King 1983, Eq. 19) that my $W^1$–$W^6$ family generalises. The paper speaks to all three of my channels structurally and to my welfare layer, but it is a survey of methods, not an opportunity-inequality decomposition, so its direct contribution is machinery and provenance, not a benchmark decomposition result.

## 3. Model and objects (map object-by-object to mine)
- **Choice set = my latent-jobs set?** Yes, conceptually. <cite index="6-1">In the RURO model the household chooses among opportunities ("jobs") defined by hours $h$, wage rates $w$ (which can vary across jobs for the same household), and other analyst-unobserved attributes $j$.</cite> This is exactly my latent-jobs set. A key difference from the DC model is stressed: <cite index="6-0">the RURO utility function is directly specified as stochastic, with the random component interpreted in McFadden's terms as analyst-unobserved job/match characteristics</cite> (commuting time, required skill are their examples) — i.e. RUM-interpretation of $\varepsilon$, *not* optimisation error.
- **Deterministic utility = my preference utility $v$?** Yes. Their systematic utility $v(f(wh,I),h)$ over net income and hours is my preference block, except my $v$ is a **Box–Cox in consumption and leisure** with demographic taste-shifters and gender; theirs is left general in the survey, and the specific functional form they exhibit for *welfare* is a different object (the common utility $V$, see §6 below).   - **wage (ability)** → their $g_2(w)$, specified **log-normal** → maps to my **ability** sub-block (the wage technology). ## 4. Estimation method
- **Likelihood and estimator:** Conditional/multinomial logit choice probabilities derived from i.i.d. Type-I extreme-value $\varepsilon$. For RURO the per-(w,h) probability is the systematic-utility exponential **weighted by the opportunity density** (their Eq. 6, discrete version):
  $$\varphi(w,h)=\frac{\exp\{v(f(wh,I),h)\}\,p(w,h)}{\sum_{(x,y)\in B}\exp\{v(f(xy,I),y)\}\,p(x,y)}.$$
- **Choice-set construction:** Both continuous (Eq. 5, whole positive $(w,h)$ quadrant) and **discrete sampled-alternative** (Eq. 6) versions. <cite index="9-1">In RURO models the opportunity set is unknown because the opportunity density must be estimated, and the opportunity set used in estimation and simulation can be interpreted as a sample drawn from an unknown population, so the sampling method is a relevant issue.</cite> Grid size not fixed by the survey; my 901 (couples)/101 (singles) resolution is my own choice.
- **Proposal / sampling density:** They sample $(w,h)$ from a **pre-estimated density $q(w,h)$** and re-weight (Aaberge et al. 1995, 1999; Ben-Akiva–Lerman 1985). This is the direct antecedent of my proposal $\pi$.
- **Prior/proposal correction — is $\log(\text{prior})$ subtracted, always well defined?** **Yes, explicitly (their Eq. 11):**
  $$\varphi(w,h)=\frac{\exp\{v(f(wh,I),h)\}\,p(w,h)/q(w,h)}{\sum_{(x,y)\in \hat B}\exp\{v(f(xy,I),y)\}\,p(x,y)/q(x,y)},$$
  <cite index="9-0">where alternatives are sampled from a pre-estimated density $q(w,h)$ and Equation 6 is re-weighted following Ben-Akiva and Lerman (1985).</cite> The correction is the ratio $p/q$ (equivalently, in log space, $\log p - \log q$); in my pipeline the analogue is the per-row $-\log\pi$ column. The survey does not flag well-definedness conditions explicitly, but the ratio form requires $q>0$ on sampled nodes — satisfied by construction when nodes are drawn from $q$. **[verify: my $-\log\pi$ is the $\log q$ piece; the $\log p$ piece is inside my estimated $g$. The mapping $p/q \leftrightarrow g \cdot \pi^{-1}$ is exact in structure but I should confirm my code's sign/placement against this.]**
- **Normalisation and scale:** Standard logit scale normalisation (utility identified up to the EV scale); the survey notes behaviour is invariant to monotonic transforms of utility, which is *the* motivation for the separate common-utility welfare object (§6). No further scale discussion on supplied pages.
- **Numerical method / starting values / multistart:** Not detailed in the survey. [verify in primary RURO sources Aaberge–Colombino–Strøm 1999.]
- **What pins down preferences separately from the opportunity mechanism?** The survey's answer is **functional-form / distributional structure**, not an exclusion restriction: the multiplicative separation of $\exp(v)$ and $p(w,h)$, plus the parametric forms (uniform-with-peaks hours, log-normal wages), plus the requirement that densities depend on characteristics in specified ways. This is exactly the identification basis I rely on, and exactly the basis a "your decomposition is mechanical" referee will attack — see §8.
- **Verdict — reusable for my RURO/JAX pipeline?** **Yes, directly, as the structural and provenance reference.** Reuse: (i) Eq. 6 as my choice-probability core; (ii) Eq. 11 as the provenance of my $-\log\pi$ correction (name the step: sampling-of-alternatives re-weighting, Ben-Akiva–Lerman); (iii) Eq. 7 factorisation as the literature antecedent of my access/ability channel split. **No** for the continuous version (Eq. 5) and **no** for their specific welfare functional forms as-is (I generalise them).

## 4b. Proposal / sampling-of-alternatives correction
- **Exactly how alternatives are sampled and corrected:** Draw $(w,h)$ from pre-estimated $q(w,h)$; form the likelihood as the $p/q$-weighted logit (Eq. 11). The opportunity set used in estimation **is** a sample from the unknown opportunity population, so each drawn alternative carries its own weight via $p(x,y)/q(x,y)$ in both numerator (chosen) and denominator (all sampled).
- **Partly individualised or common?** The survey states only that **all densities $p_1,g_1,g_2$ and the wage density can depend on household/job characteristics** (Eq. 7 discussion). It does **not** specify which channels are individualised vs common — that granularity is mine, established by my own proposal audit (wage and occupation individualised; hours and employment common). So: the *capacity* for individualisation is explicit-in-source; the *specific* common-vs-individualised split of my pipeline is **not established by this source** and must not be attributed to it.
- **McFadden-style?** Yes — the re-weighting is the Ben-Akiva–Lerman correction for sampling of alternatives within the McFadden conditional-logit family.
- **Each drawn alternative carries its own log-prior?** Yes (the $p/q$ ratio is per-alternative). This matches my per-row prior column and my statement that "the draws constituting the grids *are* the importance-sampling proposal sample, each alternative carrying its own log-prior, so no separate McFadden correction is required."
- **Relation to my IS welfare integrator and proposal-individualisation concern:** The paper grounds the *existence and form* of the correction; it does **not** address importance-sampling **effective sample size**, weight degeneracy, or the welfare-integration use of the draws. My ESS diagnostic and redraw cross-check are extensions beyond anything in this source.

## 5. Opportunity mechanism [MOST IMPORTANT — split by channel]
The opportunity mechanism is a **density $p(w,h)$ over jobs**, estimated, interpreted as the population of available jobs from which the analyst observes a sample. It is **not** offer probabilities in a search sense and **not** a reservation-wage rule; it is a density weighting in the choice probability (Eq. 6). It varies with observable circumstances because all its component densities may depend on household/job characteristics (Eq. 7 discussion). Functional forms, by channel:

- **access (hours / market-participation):**
  - *Market participation:* $p_1$ = density/probability mass of market jobs ($h>0$); $1-p_1$ at $h=0$ (Eq. 7). Maps to my market/participation access sub-block.
  - *Hours availability:* $g_1(h)$ = conditional hours density, **uniform-with-peaks**, with estimable peaks at part-time and full-time. Maps to my hours-availability access sub-block. The "dummies refinement" (Eqs. 2–3, 9–10) is the discrete device that implements peaks/availability via alternative-specific terms; in RURO these terms carry a **structural opportunity interpretation** (Eq. 10: $\gamma_0=\ln J + A_0$, $\gamma_\ell=\ln(J_\ell/J)+A_\ell$, with $J,J_\ell$ = numbers of available alternatives) rather than a pure taste interpretation.
- **ability (the wage technology):**
  - $g_2(w)$ = conditional wage density, **log-normal**. This is the source's "wage opportunity" object and the antecedent of my ability sub-block. **Caveat (derived-by-analogy):** a log-normal wage *opportunity density* is not the same parameterisation as my returns-to-education-and-experience-plus-$\sigma$ wage technology; the role (governing which wages are available) is the same, the functional form is not identical. Do not cite this source for "returns to education in the opportunity density"; cite it for "the wage-opportunity density $g_2(w)$, log-normal, possibly characteristic-dependent."
- **occupation:** **No occupation channel in the baseline $p(w,h)$.** Occupation/sector appears only as a *generalisation* (§2.6) — adding sector-of-employment or occupational choice as an extra choice dimension (public/private sector examples). The source uses **"sector of employment"** and **"occupational choice"** language; **this is the source's framing and must not be read as my occupation-as-access (`loc4`) object.** My occupation channel is an extension relative to this survey's standard model. No sector/industry conflation in the source's baseline because it has neither; the conflation risk is purely on the import side (me reading their "sector" as my occupation).
- **Random-opportunity framing:** The source **does** treat the opportunity set as *random from the analyst's viewpoint*. <cite index="6-2">Because the analyst does not know which or how many jobs are in the household's opportunity set, the opportunity set can be seen as random from the analyst's viewpoint, and the number or density of jobs of each type plays a crucial role.</cite> **This is the "random opportunities" reading my design explicitly removes** — in my JMP opportunities are *deterministic* and the "RO" is estimation machinery. Flag every time I cite RURO: the source's randomness-of-the-set language is a modelling-of-analyst-ignorance device, which I reinterpret as a deterministic feasible set estimated up to the density. Do not let the source's wording reintroduce a random-opportunities claim into my paper.
- **If no explicit mechanism:** N/A — the mechanism is explicit and central.

## 6. Welfare object — and its place on my $W^1$–$W^6$ map
- **Does the paper compute welfare?** It surveys welfare/social-evaluation method (§5.5) and reports optimal-tax and policy-simulation welfare results, but the welfare **apparatus** is the relevant content.
- **Money-metric? equivalent income? CV/EV? log-sum?** It surveys **all of these**:
  - *Money-metric via a common utility function.* To solve the interpersonal-comparability problem created by ordinality, they adopt a **common utility function** $V$ representing the social planner's preferences, distinct from the household-specific positive utility $U$ used for behaviour. <cite index="24-3,24-4">A common utility function is proposed as a tool for interpersonal comparisons because it contains interpersonal comparability of both welfare levels and differences, capturing the social planner's preferences while the individual utility functions capture only consumption/leisure preferences.</cite> Their specific $V$ (their Eq. 17) is a two-term Box–Cox-type form in income $y$ and leisure $L$, with couples' incomes divided by $\sqrt 2$ (Eq. 18). **This common-utility object is the conceptual parent of my preference-respecting equivalent income, but note the difference: their $V$ removes preference heterogeneity (a single planner utility), whereas my $W^k$ family is preference-*respecting* (each household evaluated under its own indifference map).** Do not conflate.
  - *King (1983) money-metric / equivalent income.* They present King's construction explicitly (their Eq. 19): a comparable money-metric index $\omega_i$ defined by $V^*(w_R,\omega_i,Z_R)=V^*(w_i,I_i,Z_i)$ — the income that, at a **reference** budget/characteristics, yields the household's attained utility. <cite index="26-0,26-1">King (1983) represents different preferences by characteristics $Z_i$ within a common parametric utility; the comparable money-metric index $\omega_i$ is defined by equating reference-budget utility to the household's attained maximum utility.</cite> **This is the direct antecedent of my equivalent-income inversion** — my $W^k$ are exactly "income that equates attained utility to a measure-specific reference, under own preferences," differing from King in that I respect each household's own preferences and span a *family* of references.
  - *Preference-respecting metrics.* They explicitly point to the alternative I actually use: <cite index="25-4">Alternative preference-respecting welfare approaches are attributed to Piacquadio (2017), Fleurbaey (2008), and Fleurbaey and Maniquet (2006), the latter applied in labour-supply analyses by Bargain et al. (2013) and Decoster and Haan (2015).</cite> Crucially they record the **sensitivity-to-the-metric** finding that motivates my family-as-headline design: <cite index="25-4">Decoster and Haan (2015) acknowledge that the choice of a preference-respecting welfare metric can significantly affect the welfare evaluation and depends on how much emphasis the metric places on willingness-to-work, so a work-averse or work-loving individual is favoured depending on the metric.</cite>
- **Universal vs constrained feasible set?** The money-metric/equivalent-income constructions here are defined against a **reference budget** $(w_R,I_R)$ and reference characteristics $Z_R$ (King) or a common utility (Aaberge–Colombino). They are **not** defined as my ex-ante inclusive value over the household's feasible opportunity set. The survey's welfare objects are essentially **ex-post / indirect-utility-at-the-budget** objects, not ex-ante log-sum-over-the-feasible-set objects.
- **Reference price / preference / bundle / set:** Reference budget and reference characteristics (King); a single planner utility (Aaberge–Colombino common-utility). My measure-specific references ($\bar A$, $\mathcal J$, laissez-faire, etc.) are richer and are mine.
- **Discrete-choice subtleties (log-sum, selection, Hicksian/Marshallian, integration over heterogeneity, ex-ante/ex-post):** The survey's **policy-simulation** side uses the inclusive-value / expected-choice machinery (it describes computing the **expected chosen value** via estimated choice probabilities, and the alternative of simulating systematic + random utility and taking the argmax with random components held fixed across regimes — their two procedures in §5). But the survey's **welfare-evaluation** objects (Eqs. 17–19) are framed at the level of the indirect/common utility, **not** as a log-sum equivalent income. So the source contains the *ingredients* of an ex-ante object (inclusive value appears on the simulation side) but does **not** assemble the ex-ante money-metric equivalent income I build. **My ex-ante inclusive-value equivalent income is therefore an extension, not something to attribute to this source.**
- **Locate on my $W^1$–$W^6$ map:** The source does **NOT** contain $W^1$–$W^6$. It contains (i) the King equivalent-income template that my measures *instantiate*, and (ii) an explicit pointer to Fleurbaey–Maniquet preference-respecting metrics, which is the tradition my family sits in. The closest *single* correspondence is **King's reference-budget money-metric ≈ the general form of which each $W^k$ is a specific reference choice**; but the source has no compensation–responsibility spectrum, no Ind-$y$/Ind-$A$ classification, and no menu. **Do not claim the source maps to any specific $W^k$ or to the spectrum; cite it only for the King template and the metric-sensitivity motivation.**

## 7. Inequality / decomposition content [three-way where relevant]
- **Inequality indices:** The survey lays out, with equations, the standard families:
  - **Atkinson (1970)** equally-distributed-equivalent and the $\theta$-parameterised inequality measure (their Eqs. 20–23): $W(F)=\int u(x)dF(x)$, EDE $\xi(F)=u^{-1}(W(F))$, $I(F)=1-\xi(F)/\mu$, with the constant-elasticity family. Inequality-aversion parameter $\theta\ge 0$.
  - **Rank-dependent / generalised-Gini** (Weymark 1981; Yaari 1988), their Eq. 24: $W^*_k(F)=\int_0^1 p_k(t)F^{-1}(t)dt$ with concave weight $p_k$ and aversion parameter $k$, and the associated inequality index $C_k=1-W^*_k/\mu$ (Eq. 25). The "dual" approach; Gini is the canonical case.
  - **Kolm (1976)** absolute (translation-invariant) index, their Eq. 26: $K=\tfrac1\alpha\ln\int\exp\{-\alpha(x-\mu)\}dF(x)$, with social welfare $W=\mu-K$.
  - <cite index="29-0">The Atkinson and rank-dependent indices are invariant with respect to multiplicative constants, whereas the Kolm index is invariant with respect to additive constants, and practical rescaling considerations can drive the choice between multiplicative and additive forms.</cite>
- **Decomposition rule (Shapley/Shorrocks/factor/subgroup/RIF/Owen)?** **NONE.** The paper presents inequality *measurement* and *social-welfare* families but performs **no decomposition** of inequality into sources, factors, or subgroups. There is **no Shapley, no Shorrocks, no factor decomposition** in this paper. **Do not cite this source for any decomposition method.**
- **Counterfactual construction (what is equalised/neutralised/held fixed/zeroed):** N/A — no decomposition counterfactuals. The closest is the **policy-simulation comparative-statics** logic (compare two equilibria induced by two tax rules, random components held fixed across regimes), which is a *policy* counterfactual, not a *source-of-inequality* counterfactual.
- **Order-independence / path-independence / exhaustiveness:** N/A for decomposition. (The inequality indices themselves have standard axiomatic properties, but that is index theory, not decomposition.)
- **Verdict — reusable for my three-way access/ability/preference Shapley–Shorrocks split?** **Only the inequality-index layer is reusable** — Atkinson, generalised-Gini/rank-dependent, Kolm — as the $I(\cdot)$ applied to my welfare distribution $\Omega^k$. The **decomposition rule itself must come from elsewhere** (Shorrocks 2013; my own Shapley equalisation design). This source is **not** two-way or three-way; it is **zero-way on decomposition**. To get from here to my three-way split I must add the entire equalisation-and-Shapley apparatus, which this paper does not contain.

## 8. Identification and the separation of preferences from opportunities [STRICT]
- **What identifies tastes vs constraints here?** The separation rests on **functional-form and distributional structure**, not on an exclusion restriction, panel, or external instrument:
  1. the **multiplicative separation** in the choice probability (Eq. 6): systematic utility $\exp(v)$ times opportunity density $p(w,h)$;
  2. **parametric forms** for the opportunity densities — uniform-with-peaks $g_1(h)$, log-normal $g_2(w)$, market mass $p_1$ — with specified characteristic-dependence;
  3. the **RUM interpretation of $\varepsilon$** (unobserved job attributes), which makes $h=0$ an optimal choice and lets the opportunity density absorb availability differences.
- **Ability vs access within the opportunity side?** The source does **not** draw an ability/access distinction. The *material* for it exists ($g_2(w)$ wage density vs $g_1(h),p_1$ hours/participation), but the source does not interpret $g_2$ as "ability" or $g_1,p_1$ as "access." That interpretation is **mine** (derived-by-analogy). **Do not attribute an ability/access identification argument to this paper.**
- **Exact source of identification named?** **Functional-form restriction + distributional assumptions on the opportunity densities and on $\varepsilon$ (Type-I EV).** No exclusion restriction; no external opportunity shifter (local unemployment, vacancies); the survey relies on the parametric opportunity density. This is candidly the same basis my baseline uses.

## 10. Estimators, theorems, or formal results
For each, statement (LaTeX), assumptions, technique, reusability verdict.

1. **RURO choice probability, discrete (Eq. 6).**
   $$\varphi(w,h)=\frac{\exp\{v(f(wh,I),h)\}\,p(w,h)}{\sum_{(x,y)\in B}\exp\{v(f(xy,I),y)\}\,p(x,y)}.$$
   *Assumptions:* i.i.d. Type-I EV $\varepsilon$; opportunity density $p$ over $(w,h)$; net income via $f$. *Technique:* (i) RUM with EV shocks → conditional logit; (ii) weight systematic-utility exponential by opportunity density $p$; (iii) discrete version of the Dagsvik (1994) continuous result; (iv) interpret $p$ as job-type availability counts. *Reusability:* **Yes — this is my choice-probability core.**

2. **Opportunity-density factorisation (Eq. 7).**
   $$p(w,h)=\begin{cases}p_1\,g_1(h)\,g_2(w) & h>0\\ 1-p_1 & h=0\end{cases}$$
   *Assumptions:* $g_1$ uniform-with-peaks; $g_2$ log-normal; all densities may depend on characteristics. *Technique:* factor market participation × hours × wage. *Reusability:* **Yes — literature antecedent of my access (hours/market) vs ability (wage) channel split.** Reuse with the caveat that my wage channel is a return-to-characteristics technology, not a bare log-normal.

3. **Sampling-of-alternatives re-weighting (Eq. 11).**
   $$\varphi(w,h)=\frac{\exp\{v(f(wh,I),h)\}\,p(w,h)/q(w,h)}{\sum_{(x,y)\in \hat B}\exp\{v(f(xy,I),y)\}\,p(x,y)/q(x,y)}.$$
   *Assumptions:* alternatives drawn from $q$; $q>0$ on sampled nodes; Ben-Akiva–Lerman correction. *Technique:* importance-style re-weighting of the conditional logit over a sampled choice set. *Reusability:* **Yes — provenance of my mandatory $-\log\pi$ correction.** (My ESS/redraw extensions are not here.)

4. **DC-reduction with structural ASCs (Colombino 2013; Eqs. 8–10).**
   $$\gamma_0=\ln J + A_0,\qquad \gamma_\ell=\ln(J_\ell/J)+A_\ell.$$
   *Assumptions:* opportunity density specified uniform-with-peaks; $J,J_\ell$ = counts of available alternatives. *Technique:* show RURO ≡ DC-with-dummies, but with dummy coefficients carrying opportunity-count meaning. *Reusability:* **Maybe (as a robustness/interpretation aid)** — useful to argue my access dummies have a structural (availability) reading; **caution** because it is the one spot where an opportunity term looks like a utility ASC.

5. **King money-metric / equivalent income (Eq. 19).**
   $$V^*(w_R,\omega_i,Z_R)=V^*(w_i,I_i,Z_i).$$
   *Assumptions:* common parametric utility with preference-characteristics $Z_i$; reference budget $(w_R,I_R)$ and reference characteristics $Z_R$. *Technique:* invert utility to the reference-budget income that reproduces attained utility. *Reusability:* **Yes (form), adapt (object)** — the inversion logic of my $W^k$; I replace the indirect utility with the ex-ante inclusive value and respect own preferences rather than imposing a common utility.

6. **Atkinson EDE/inequality family (Eqs. 20–23); rank-dependent/Gini family (Eqs. 24–25); Kolm (Eq. 26).** *Reusability:* **Yes** as the inequality functionals applied to my $\Omega^k$; choose multiplicative (Atkinson/Gini) vs additive (Kolm) per invariance needs. **No** decomposition content attached.

7. **Computational optimal-tax / social-planner program (Eq. 16).** *Statement:* maximise $W(U_1,\dots,U_N)$ over tax parameters $\vartheta$ s.t. each household's $(c_n,h_n,j_n)$ is a utility-maximising RURO choice and the budget constraint $\sum(w_nh_n+I_n-f)\ge R$. *Reusability:* **No** for my JMP (I am not doing optimal taxation), but useful as context for how RURO welfare objects feed a social-welfare functional.
