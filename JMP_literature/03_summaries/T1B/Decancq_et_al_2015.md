## 1. One-paragraph relevance to my JMP
This is a foundational reference for the **welfare object** of my paper: it derives, axiomatically, the *equivalent income* as the unique preference-respecting money-metric of individual well-being, defined as the income that, combined with a reference value on all non-income dimensions, leaves the individual indifferent to her actual situation (explicit-in-source, eq. (1), p. 1089). It speaks to the **preference** channel only: its entire concern is respecting heterogeneous $R_i$ in interpersonal comparison, and it has *no* labour-supply choice set, *no* opportunity mechanism, and therefore says nothing about my **access** or **ability** channels. Its direct use to me is (i) the normative justification for using a preference-respecting equivalent income rather than raw consumption or SWB, and (ii) the explicit demonstration that the *choice of reference value* for non-income dimensions is a normative act that changes who is identified as worst off — the abstract analogue of my measure-menu $W^1$–$W^6$ idea, though this paper characterises **one** equivalent-income family with a flexible reference, not my six-measure responsibility spectrum.

- **Key variables (life dimensions $\ell$):** equivalised expenditures per consumption unit, self-assessed health, housing quality, (un)employment, wage arrears; conditioning/scaling variables $Z$ include gender, education, marital status, age, minority, rural, year (explicit-in-source, pp. 1094, 1101). Equivalence scale = square root of household size (explicit-in-source, n.23).
- **Budget-set construction:** N/A — there is no budget set or labour-supply choice problem in this paper. Welfare is computed from a *satisfaction equation*, not from a structural choice model.
- **Transport to my France pooled 2015–2017 EUROMOD cross-section:** Poorly, at the data/method level. **Not established** that the SWB-based preference-recovery route transports to my setting, and it is *not* my route: I recover preferences from a structural discrete-choice labour-supply model on EUROMOD, not from a life-satisfaction regression. Conversely they have **no** labour-supply choice set, **no** wage/hours/occupation opportunity structure, and **no** decomposition. The transportable content is conceptual/normative, not empirical.

## 3. Model and objects (map object-by-object to mine)
- **Choice set = my latent-jobs set?** No. There is no choice set and no latent jobs. The primitive is a life-dimension vector $\ell_i=(y,q)$ with $y$ income/expenditures and $q$ non-income dimensions (explicit-in-source, p. 1089).
- **Deterministic utility = my preference utility $v$?** Partially analogous. They posit a preference ordering $R_i$ over $\ell_i$, represented by a satisfaction function $S_i(\ell_i)$ under a **consistency assumption** that $S_i$ is *a* representation of $R_i$ (explicit-in-source, p. 1084). This is an ordinal preference object akin to my Box–Cox utility $v$ in role, but defined over (income, quality-of-life) rather than over (consumption, leisure, children-time) and **not** estimated from choices. Derived-by-analogy: their $R_i$ ↔ my preference block; the mapping is loose because their argument set and identification source differ.
- **Explicit opportunity / availability mechanism analogous to my $g$?** **No.** There is no $g$, no offer density, no feasibility restriction. Consequently there is **no separation of hours / wage (ability) / market / occupation channels**. This is the central limitation for my purposes: the paper is preference-side only.
- **Budget map = my EUROMOD disposable income?** No budget map. Income/expenditures enter as a life dimension $y$, measured as equivalised household expenditures (chosen over income as a better proxy for permanent income; explicit-in-source, pp. 1093–1094), not as a tax-benefit function of labour supply.
- **Job attribute entering BOTH utility and opportunity?** N/A — there is no opportunity mechanism, so the double-entry flag cannot arise. (Note for contrast with my design: my discipline keeps occupation in $g$ only; this paper has no such object to discipline.)

- **Proposal/sampling density and $-\log(\text{prior})$ correction:** **N/A — there is no sampling-of-alternatives correction in this paper.** Do not attribute any McFadden-style proposal correction to it.
- **Normalisation/scale:** ordered-logit thresholds and the usual scale normalisation of a latent-index ordered model [verify thresholds not reported in the excerpted tables]; individual fixed effects $\alpha_i$, time dummies $\mu_t$, and the disturbance are treated as *scaling* and **drop out** of the equivalent income (explicit-in-source, eq. (5) and surrounding text, pp. 1091–1092).
- **Numerical method / starting values / multistart:** [verify — not detailed in source beyond citing the Jones–Schurer approximation].

## 5. Opportunity mechanism  [MOST IMPORTANT]
**There is no explicit opportunity mechanism in this paper.** It uses, in effect, a *common universal* description of life situations: every individual's situation is the triplet $(\ell,R,s)$, and there is no feasible-set object, no offer probability, no reservation-wage/participation restriction, and no household-specific availability of jobs, hours, wages, or occupations (explicit-in-source throughout §I–II).
- **access** (hours / participation / region / year / occupation offers): **not modelled.** The closest thing is *regional reference effects* — mean regional unemployment and mean regional expenditures are entered into the satisfaction equation as **scaling/social-comparison** terms, and higher regional unemployment lowers satisfaction via an informational channel about future employment prospects (explicit-in-source, pp. 1095). This is a *scaling* device, not a feasible-set/access object, and it does **not** map to my access channel. Do not read regional unemployment here as my access shifter.
- **ability** (wage technology, returns to education/experience, residual productivity): **not modelled.** There is no wage equation and no returns-to-schooling estimation in the opportunity sense.
- **occupation as availability vs other:** occupation appears only as **"occupational status/prestige,"** which the authors treat (in the benchmark) as a *scaling*/aspiration variable, not as a life dimension and not as availability (explicit-in-source, p. 1094). **Flag:** this is neither my `loc4` access object nor an industry/sector object; no sector/industry conflation occurs because no such variable is used.
- **Functional form of the (absent) mechanism:** N/A.
- **Cost to my access/ability/preference decomposition of this omission:** the paper can speak only to the **preference** vertex of my three-way cut. Used uncritically it would license a *two-vertex world* (income vs preference-respecting-quality-of-life) with the entire opportunity side collapsed into the income dimension $y$ and the rest folded into preferences/scaling — exactly the conflation my paper exists to undo. It is therefore a welfare/normative citation, never an opportunity-mechanism citation.

## 6. Welfare object — and its place on my $W^1$–$W^6$ map
- **Does it compute welfare?** Yes — its central object is a **money-metric, preference-respecting equivalent income** (explicit-in-source, p. 1083 and eq. (1) p. 1089).
- **Money-metric / equivalent income / EV-CV / inclusive value?** It is an **equivalent income** in the King (1983) / money-metric-utility tradition (explicit-in-source, p. 1083). It is **not** an expected-maximum / log-sum / inclusive-value object — there is no discrete-choice aggregation here. It is **not** framed as Hicksian EV/CV against prices; it is an indifference-based income equivalent against a *reference quality-of-life vector*.
- **Universal vs constrained feasible set?** Defined over a **universal** description of life situations $(\ell,R,s)$; there is **no constrained feasible set**. This is the key mismatch with my *constrained, ex-ante* object.
- **Reference used:** the non-income dimensions are set to a reference vector $q^*(y;R)$, the **preference-optimal** quality-of-life given income — endogenous to the individual's own preferences (eq. (1), p. 1089). The paper contrasts this with the *traditional* exogenous reference $\tilde q$ (eq. (2), p. 1090). Empirically they operationalise references as: perfect health; 90th-percentile housing; the no-wage-arrears state; and, for unemployment where groups disagree, **each sociodemographic group's own most-preferred state** (explicit-in-source, p. 1096).
- **Discrete-choice subtleties (log-sum, chosen-alternative selection, Hicksian vs Marshallian, integration over heterogeneity, ex-ante vs ex-post):** mostly **N/A** — there is no discrete choice. The relevant subtlety they *do* handle is removing scaling: fixed effects, time dummies, direct $Z$ effects ($\delta$), and the disturbance all drop out of $y^*$ (eq. (5), p. 1091–1092). There is no ex-ante/ex-post distinction because there is no choice over feasible sets; their object is closest in spirit to an **ex-post, realised-situation** evaluation. Do **not** present this as my constrained ex-ante inclusive-value object.
- **Location on my $W^1$–$W^6$ map:** **Derived-by-analogy, with caution.** This paper's equivalent income is the *generic* preference-respecting money-metric that my whole family specialises; it is not itself any one of $W^1$–$W^6$, and **the source does not contain the $W^1$–$W^6$ taxonomy** (that taxonomy is from the companion Haydar–Maniquet theory paper, not from this PDF). The mappable idea is narrower: their *reference-value choice* is the lever that, in my design, becomes the Ind-$y$/Ind-$A$ reference choice distinguishing the measures. Their endogenous-reference $q^*(y;R)$ is a "best on non-income dimensions, conditional on income" construction; in *loose* analogy that resembles a "best in own situation" reference, but it is over generic non-income quality-of-life, not over a job set, so it does **not** equal my $W^1$ (preferred job in own set) — assert no identity.
- **Verdict:** **adaptable** (the equivalent-income inversion and the normative axioms), **incompatible as-is** (the universal-set, SWB-identified, no-opportunity construction). Cite for the welfare *concept* and its *axiomatic justification*; never for the opportunity-sensitive or constrained-set features.

## 6b. Inclusive value and money-metric inversion
- **Inclusive value (log-sum) as welfare core?** **No.** The paper does not use an expected-maximum/log-sum. Do not attribute an inclusive-value welfare core to it.
- **Welfare by inverting an own-utility map to a money figure (1-D solve)?** **Yes, in spirit.** Equivalent income $y^*$ solves the indifference $(y,q)\,I\,(y^*,q^*(y^*;R))$ (eq. (1), p. 1089); under the log-linear satisfaction specification this has the **closed form** eq. (5): $y^*_{it}=y_{it}\exp\!\big[\big((\vartheta+\Lambda Z_{it})/(\beta+\Gamma Z_{it})\big)'(q_{it}-q^*_i)\big]$ (explicit-in-source, p. 1092). Note: this closed form is a property of their *functional form*, and it **does** stay within the individual's own preferences (it uses her own MRS), so it is **not** the kind of preference-bypassing shortcut my spec forbids — it is the analytic solution of the own-utility indifference, which is exactly what my one-dimensional bracketing root-solve computes numerically. Reusable as a sanity-check/closed-form benchmark for the inversion logic; the *integration over shocks* question is N/A here.
- **Expectation over extreme-value shocks analytic vs simulated?** N/A — no shocks/no discrete-choice expectation in this paper.

## 8. Identification and the separation of preferences from opportunities  [STRICT]

## 9. Key results and magnitudes
- Equivalent income is **weakly correlated** with equivalised expenditures: Spearman $\rho = 0.48$; highest correlation is with the objective HDI-style measure, $\rho = 0.64$; correlation with subjective satisfaction is low, $\rho = 0.25$ (explicit-in-source, pp. 1096–1098; year 2000 cross-tabs).
- Composition of the worst-off **changes by more than half** moving from expenditures-only (Set I) to equivalent income with health added (Set II); the worst-off set is then fairly stable across added dimensions until fixed effects (Set XI) and the disturbance (Set XII) are added — strongly suggesting the equivalent-income picture is **robust to the choice of life dimensions** but **very different from both the expenditures and the satisfaction pictures** (explicit-in-source, p. 1097).
- Subjectively least-satisfied individuals are **not** the most deprived on objective dimensions: they have larger expenditures, better health, nicer houses, are younger, better educated, more likely male (explicit-in-source, p. 1099).
- Satisfaction-equation estimates (Table A2, p. 1101): log expenditures coefficient 0.314, self-assessed health 0.432, housing 0.284 (all significant); direct effects of unemployment, wage arrears, higher education **insignificant**; education enters strongly through *interactions* (e.g. higher-education × unemployed $-0.468$), implying education shifts preferences, not just aspirations (explicit-in-source, pp. 1094, 1101). N = 40,120; pseudo-$R^2$ = 0.082.
- **Benchmarking value for me:** the headline transportable fact is the **divergence of worst-off identification across metrics** — a preference-respecting metric reshuffles the bottom relative to income and to SWB. This makes it *plausible* that my $W^1$–$W^6$ menu will likewise reshuffle the welfare distribution across stances; it is corroborating motivation, **not** a magnitude I can port (their numbers are SWB-Russia-specific and contain no opportunity content).

## 10. Estimators, theorems, or formal results
- **Characterisation result (informal "easily proved" claim, not a numbered theorem).** Statement (near-verbatim paraphrase in LaTeX): combining the **personal-preference principle** and the **weak dominance principle** characterises the well-being ordering that compares individuals by their **equivalent incomes**, where $y^*$ solves
  $$ (y,q)\; I\; \big(y^*,\,q^*(y^*;R)\big). $$
  (explicit-in-source, p. 1089, eq. (1) and the proof sketch on pp. 1089–1090.)
  - **Assumptions:** consistency of $S_i$ with $R_i$; preferences $R_i$ over $\ell=(y,q)$; existence of a preference-optimal $q^*(y;R)$ (the paper notes $y^*$ may fail to exist for some monotonic-unbounded cases, n.17, but the *ordering* remains well-defined).
  - **Technique (bullets):** (i) personal-preference principle ⇒ welfare comparison can be made at indifferent reference bundles; (ii) move each individual along her own indifference surface to her preference-optimal $q^*$ at an income $y^*$; (iii) weak dominance principle ⇒ at the optimal-$q$ situations, more income = higher well-being; (iv) hence rank by $y^*$.
  - **Reusability verdict:** **Yes (maybe + how)** for my **welfare layer's normative spine** — it is the citable justification that a preference-respecting money-metric (my equivalent income) is the right object and that the reference is a normative choice. **No** for my estimation or decomposition layers.
- **Auxiliary formal objects:** the satisfaction equation (eq. (4)), the MRS formula, and the closed-form equivalent income (eq. (5)) — see §6b; reusable as the closed-form analogue of my numerical inversion.
- **Properties table (Table 1, p. 1090):** equivalent income uniquely satisfies personal-preference + same-preference + weak-dominance (but not full dominance); SWB satisfies only personal-preference; objective measures satisfy only dominance. Reusable as a one-line normative justification table; **explicit-in-source.**

## 16. TL;DR for retrieval
Decancq–Fleurbaey–Schokkaert (2015) axiomatically characterise **equivalent income** — a money-metric, preference-respecting individual-welfare object — via the personal-preference and weak-dominance principles, and show on Russian SWB panel data that it reshuffles the worst-off relative to income and to subjective satisfaction; it informs my **preference** channel and my **welfare-object** justification only. It has **no** opportunity/access/ability mechanism, **no** discrete-choice/inclusive-value structure, and **no** inequality decomposition, so it grounds the *metric and its normative reading* but not my opportunity layer, my $W^1$–$W^6$ taxonomy (which is the companion theory paper's, not this source's), or my Shapley decomposition. Cite for the equivalent-income concept, the case against raw SWB, and the consequential-reference point; never for opportunity modelling, ex-ante constrained welfare, or source-decomposition.
