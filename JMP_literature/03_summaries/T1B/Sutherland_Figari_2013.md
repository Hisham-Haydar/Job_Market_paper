## 1. One-paragraph relevance to my JMP
This is the canonical methods-overview citation for **EUROMOD**, the tax-benefit calculator that produces my consumption/disposable-income object $C$ for every job package in the latent-jobs choice set, and therefore the engine behind both the build-time $c_{ij}$ and the reference-coverage EUROMOD evaluations my welfare layer requires. It speaks to **no analytical channel** (preference, ability, or access) directly: EUROMOD is a deterministic arithmetic tax-benefit engine, not a behavioural or opportunity model. EUROMOD is a static model in the sense that the arithmetic simulation of taxes and benefits abstracts from potential behavioural reactions of individuals. Its sole role for me is to convert a (wage, hours, household-characteristics) package into post-tax-and-transfer disposable income consistently across France 2015–2017.

## 2. Data, setting, and model in brief
- **Coverage:** Pan-European; one common framework spanning all EU member states. EUROMOD simulates individual and household tax liabilities and benefit entitlements according to the policy rules in place in each member state, and its distinguishing feature is that it covers all European countries within the same framework.
- **Method:** Static (non-behavioural) arithmetic microsimulation. As with most static microsimulation models, EUROMOD combines information on relevant policy rules with detailed and representative micro-data on individual and household circumstances drawn from national household income surveys.
- **Input data:** Mostly EU-SILC as released by Eurostat, with national-SILC variants used in some countries. Most EUROMOD input data are derived from the European Union Statistics on Income and Living Conditions (EU-SILC) as released by Eurostat. This is exactly my France input basis.
- **Output:** Micro-level tax/benefit components stored per household, analysable in any statistical software; work-incentive indicators (EMTRs, replacement rates, participation tax rates) are also produced.
- **One feature I do not (and need not) have:** the cross-country *swapping* and EU-wide-aggregate machinery — irrelevant to a France-baseline single-country JMP.

## 5. Opportunity mechanism
**N/A in the model sense — and load-bearing precisely because it is N/A.** EUROMOD contains **no** opportunity mechanism: it neither models feasible job sets, nor wage offers, nor hours availability, nor occupation access. It is a deterministic budget-constraint calculator: given a job package, it returns disposable income. The paper itself frames the labour-supply use of EUROMOD as a downstream layer — the static calculations produced by EUROMOD are increasingly used to derive the budget sets used in structural discrete-choice labour-supply models, which are a well-established setup to predict individual behavioural adjustments to policy changes. For my framework this places EUROMOD strictly *inside the consumption map* $c(j)$, below and separate from the access/ability opportunity density $g$. The opportunity mechanism is entirely my model's; EUROMOD supplies only the $c_{ij}$ that the preference block $v(c,\ell)$ evaluates. Conflating EUROMOD's budget-set construction with my opportunity layer would be a category error.

## 6. Welfare object
EUROMOD computes **no welfare object** — it has no preferences and no equivalent income. What it supplies is the **disposable-income concept** that my money-metric welfare consumes. The disposable income simulated by EUROMOD corresponds to market income and public pensions after taxes and social insurance contributions are deducted and cash benefits added. In my welfare layer this disposable income *is* the household consumption $C$ entering $v_i(c_j,\ell_j)$; the equivalent-income inversion, the reference choice, and the $W^1$–$W^6$ family are all built **on top of** EUROMOD output and owe nothing to EUROMOD's own (nonexistent) normative content. Two caveats from the paper bear directly on my welfare inputs: (i) baseline runs assume full take-up and full compliance, so disposable income is the *intended* (statutory) entitlement, not the realised one — EUROMOD baseline results do not comprehensively take into account non-take-up of benefits or tax evasion; the legal rules are assumed universally respected and compliance/claiming costs assumed zero, which can result in over-estimation of taxes and benefits. A France-specific non-take-up correction exists and is switchable. (ii) Contributory benefits and pensions are read from the data rather than simulated, because contribution histories are absent from cross-sectional input. **My welfare object does not sit anywhere on the $W^1$–$W^6$ map** — EUROMOD is the income engine, not a welfare stance.

## 7. Inequality / decomposition content
**N/A for my decomposition.** The paper surveys distributional/redistributive *applications* of EUROMOD (poverty, inequality, fiscal-consolidation incidence) and notes a policy-change decomposition tradition (Bargain-style structure/population/market-income decompositions) built on EUROMOD output. But EUROMOD provides **no** inequality index and **no** decomposition rule of its own, and nothing here is reusable for my three-way access/ability/preference Shapley–Shorrocks split. The Bargain-type decomposition referenced decomposes *policy effects over time* into tax-benefit-structure vs population vs market-income components — a different object from my source decomposition of welfare inequality into opportunity vs preference channels, and not extendable to it. Treat this section as: EUROMOD is the upstream income generator; the decomposition is wholly mine.

## 8. Identification and separation of preferences from opportunities
**N/A.** EUROMOD identifies nothing in my sense — it is arithmetic, not estimation. It contributes no identifying variation for separating tastes from constraints, ability from access, or preferences from opportunities. The relevance to identification is purely instrumental: EUROMOD must map job packages to $c_{ij}$ *consistently across the pooled 2015–2017 cross-sections* (consistent deflation, consistent system-year alignment) so that the consumption dimension does not inject spurious between-year variation into my estimation. That is a reproducibility/consistency requirement on the income input, not an identification contribution.

## 12. What I can cite this paper for
- As the **standard methods-overview citation for EUROMOD** as the EU tax-benefit microsimulation infrastructure (the "what EUROMOD is and does" reference).
- For the statement that EUROMOD is a **static, non-behavioural arithmetic** tax-benefit model that converts gross/market income plus characteristics into **disposable income** under each country's statutory rules.

- For the **disposable-income concept** definition that underlies my consumption object $C$.

## 13. What I should NOT cite this paper for (overclaim risks)
- **Do NOT cite it as a welfare, equivalent-income, or normative source.** EUROMOD has no preferences and computes no welfare; it sits on neither the compensation–responsibility spectrum nor the $W^1$–$W^6$ map. (welfare-object overclaim)
- **Do NOT cite it as an opportunity-mechanism source.** EUROMOD models no feasible job set, no access, no ability; conflating its budget-set construction with my access/ability opportunity density $g$ is a category error. (random-vs-deterministic and access/ability overclaim — note EUROMOD is deterministic-arithmetic, which is *compatible* with my deterministic-opportunities stance but is not itself an opportunity model)
- **Do NOT cite it for a decomposition rule.** It supplies no inequality decomposition usable for my three-way Shapley split; the Bargain-type policy-effect decomposition it surveys is a different object (two-/three-component *policy-change* decomposition, not opportunity-vs-preference welfare decomposition). (two-way-vs-three-way and object-mismatch overclaim)
- **Do NOT cite it for occupation/sector content.** EUROMOD is silent on the ISCO (`loc4`, access) vs NACE (`lindi`, sector) distinction; nothing here speaks to it.
- **Do NOT treat EUROMOD as the identity of the JMP.** It is supporting infrastructure — the income engine below the model — not the paper's contribution, method, or object.
- **Theory-paper boundary:** not applicable here (this paper has no contact with the Haydar–Maniquet characterisation), but for the record, nothing in EUROMOD's apparatus may be read into the companion theory paper's normative primitives.

## 16. TL;DR for retrieval
Sutherland & Figari (2013) is the canonical methods-overview citation for **EUROMOD**, the EU-wide **static, non-behavioural** tax-benefit microsimulation model running mostly on **EU-SILC**, which converts gross income plus household characteristics into **disposable income** under statutory rules (baseline assumes full take-up/compliance, with switchable national non-take-up corrections). For my JMP it is **data-infrastructure only**: EUROMOD is the income engine that produces the consumption object $c_{ij}$ for every latent-job package, sitting strictly *below* the model and feeding the preference block — it touches **no** channel (preference/ability/access), supplies **no** welfare object or decomposition rule, and must never be elevated into the JMP's identity. Cite it for "what EUROMOD is and the disposable-income concept"; never for welfare, opportunities, decomposition, or normative content.
