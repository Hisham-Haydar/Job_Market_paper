## MATERIAL corrections only

### 3 — Reference sensitivity

**Location:** `JMP_seminar_deck_content_v1.md`, Slide 17, speaker notes.

Replace the range discussion with:

> “Changing only the reference-preference convention on the preferred singles model moves the preference contribution from **6.30 to 10.89 per cent on the raw basis** and from **7.98 to 10.97 per cent on the coalition-consistent equivalized basis**. These paired values are reported as ranges and never averaged. The broader **3.3–11.0 per cent** envelope is across all eight rows of Table 7.1 and therefore also reflects the positive-model and equivalization choices; it is not a reference-only range. The market-side total is nearly unchanged across reference conventions — 35.51 against 35.88 per cent on the raw basis — so reference sensitivity is concentrated in the preference/environment split rather than in the environment's internal structure.”

This preserves the actual result while identifying correctly **which perturbation produces which range**. 

### 4 — Coalition-consistent equivalization

**Location:** `JMP_working_paper_for_seminar_v1.md`, §3.5, final normative paragraph.

Replace the economic justification with:

> “Household composition is part of the endowments-and-needs factor by definition, and the equalisation operator replaces that exogenous needs profile with the reference profile. Because the equivalence scale is a deterministic function of household composition, it is evaluated at the coalition-specific composition whenever the endowments-and-needs factor is replaced. The scale therefore moves with that coalition as a consequence of the definition of the factor, not in order to force the decomposition to close. Exact exhaustiveness is a consequence of this convention. Under the alternative normative object ‘equalise resources while holding each household's own needs fixed,’ the equivalence scale would remain household-specific and the resulting dispersion would be a genuine needs component requiring a different decomposition. This paper adopts the first object.”

**Location:** `JMP_seminar_deck_content_v1.md`, Backup B3.

Replace the last normative passage with:

> “The scale moves because household composition is itself part of the endowments-and-needs factor being equalised: the equivalence scale is a function of that composition and is therefore recomputed at the coalition-specific needs profile. Exact closure is a consequence of this definition, not its justification. If instead the normative object were to equalise resources while leaving needs fixed, the household's own scale would remain and the residual would be a genuine needs component requiring different accounting.”

This resolves the important economics issue. The current convention is defensible; its justification must be **factor consistency**, not \(I^{11}=0\).

## Overall assessment

The claim set is substantially stronger than I would expect from a seminar draft at this stage: the headline decomposition is traceable to the attached table; the preference/opportunity identification limitations are mostly stated with appropriate restraint; couples are not being oversold; pooled non-exhaustiveness is correctly recognized as a reference-comparability problem; and the literature positioning gives a credible answer to “what is new relative to Decoster–Haan, JJT, and the RURO tradition.” The four material issues are **interpretive rather than substantive failures of the empirical result**: the deck currently attributes too wide a range specifically to reference sensitivity; the equivalence-scale convention is justified partly by closure rather than by the economics of the needs factor; the underemployment evidence is compared too directly with a model that has no underemployment object; and several pieces of internal process language remain alongside one material overstatement about the 35-hour peak being identified as availability rather than tastes. Correct those four points and I would regard the seminar claim-to-evidence package as economically defensible on the evidence reviewed here.
