### 1. Actual empirical setting

The empirical anchor is now France, not Belgium. In the current synthesis, France is explicitly the primary estimation setting: the programme lists French EUROMOD input data for 2016 as the primary estimation sample and 2021 as secondary, with the underlying microdata identified as SRCV, the French version of EU-SILC. The most concretely documented France implementation is a RURO pipeline built around the household/couples sample, for which the synthesis reports about 4,253 households and 10,873 person-records before final filtering, with heads and partners marked by `ruro_decider`. That is the real empirical base you should use for rewriting the abstract and extended abstract.  

This also means the older Belgium-style prototype is now misaligned with the real implementation. The prototype memo still frames the first pass as Belgium, prime-age childless single women, and a very small 16-alternative job grid. That may have been a useful design simplification, but it is no longer the correct empirical anchor for the paper. The JMP should now be written as a France-based structural labor-supply paper with welfare and decomposition ambitions, while remaining distinct from the separate pure theory paper on jobs and well-being.   

### 2. France data sources and years

The France data source is SRCV, processed through the France preparation script `enh_france_data_prep.py`. The synthesis states that the programme uses France EUROMOD input data for 2016 as the primary estimation sample and 2021 as a secondary year. The file inventory also lists France EU-SILC 2016/2021 input files, EUROMOD France system years 2015/2021, EUROMOD simulated outputs such as `updated_simulated.csv` and `simulated.txt`, and checkpoint files at multiple stages.  

For writing purposes, the safe empirical statement is: French SRCV / EUROMOD-input microdata, with 2016 as the main France application and 2021 available as a secondary or robustness year. I would not yet write more precisely than that, because the files do not yet pin down, in one clean place, the final data-year / policy-year pairing for the JMP text. The inventory mentions 2016/2021 inputs and 2015/2021 EUROMOD system years, so that pairing should be locked explicitly before you finalize the abstract.  

### 3. What is already implemented in the France RURO pipeline

A substantial France RURO pipeline is already in place. The synthesis lists a full chain of France-specific scripts: `enh_france_data_prep.py` for raw France preparation, `enh_RURO_prep.py` for RURO variables, `enh_RURO_draws.py` for alternative job draws, `enh_RURO_euromod.py` for EUROMOD simulation across draws, `enh_RURO_prep_mnl_basic.py` for estimation arrays, `enh_estimate.py` for RURO estimation with L-BFGS-B, and `enh_post_estimation.py` for post-estimation work. That is enough to say the project is not at the level of vague design; it already has an implemented France structural pipeline.  

The France pipeline is also more than data cleaning. It includes operational definitions for `lma_ruro`, `ruro_decider`, wage reconstruction from monthly labor income and weekly hours, and a hybrid wage rule that uses reconstructed wages when `yem > 100` and falls back to `yivwg` otherwise. On the estimation side, the synthesis writes down a sampled-alternative RURO likelihood with McFadden correction, a conditional log-likelihood, and analytical gradients precomputed before optimization. It also documents GMM-based job-universe generation for sampled alternatives. In other words, preferences, opportunities, simulated disposable incomes, and likelihood-based estimation are all represented in code, even if the France estimates are not yet stable.   

### 4. What modelling decisions are already fixed

In the current implementation, several modelling choices are already fixed enough to treat as the baseline code design. The sample is household-based, not person-by-person; the current canonical household definition is an opposite-sex couple with one partner; head identification uses the EUROMOD simulated-output definition rather than the raw-input fallback; the main age window is 16 to 65; full-time students are excluded; households with additional eligible non-head, non-partner members are dropped; and abnormal employee hours or wages are filtered. France is treated as a cross-section, unlike the auxiliary German pooled panel.  

On the structural side, the current code design uses wage discretisation into 20 vigintiles and hours discretisation into 4-hour intervals, with a hierarchy from `job` to richer objects such as `jobloc`, `joblind`, and `jobtot`. Preferences are modeled with Box-Cox utility in consumption and leisure, with an additional leisure-leisure interaction term for couples. The opportunity mechanism is factored into a piecewise hours component and a log-normal Mincer-type wage component. Alternatives are sampled from a GMM-based universe and corrected using the McFadden sampling adjustment. The optimizer is L-BFGS-B with analytical gradients, and the identifying restriction that non-labor income enters utility but not the opportunity density is already stated. The current welfare layer names AEI as the leading well-being measure, with a second reference-preference measure as robustness.   

### 5. What remains unresolved

The first unresolved issue is estimation stability. The synthesis is explicit that the French RURO runs have produced pathological estimates, including a negative consumption coefficient for singles and an implausibly large wage standard deviation. It also states that large gradient norms remain at convergence and that the root cause has not yet been pinned down: it could be a normalization problem, a specification problem, a data problem, or a numerical optimization problem. This is the single biggest reason not to write the abstract as if stable France estimates already exist.  

The second unresolved issue is internal consistency of the pipeline. France sample construction currently defines `lma_ruro` from `les ∈ {3,5,7}`, which includes unemployed and inactive individuals, while the “modelling decisions already taken” section says that the current labour-market eligibility rule excludes `les = 5` and explicitly notes that unemployment treatment was inconsistent across earlier code versions. The wage pipeline also contains two different work thresholds: `yem > 100` in the hybrid wage rule and `yem > 500` in the Heckman selection stage. In addition, the synthesis calls for reconciliation of the job-construction algorithm across scripts, validation of self-employment exclusion, and further checking of household head/partner merges. Those are not cosmetic issues; they affect what exactly the France model is estimating.   

The third unresolved issue is the opportunity and welfare layer. The XGBoost ability-set module performs poorly and, more importantly, conflates ability with local availability. The `gsur` job-quality component exists in the France pipeline, but its exact identification role is still described as open. AEI is the leading welfare candidate in the current code, but the synthesis also notes that the reference non-work option is not yet fully modeled. Finally, the decomposition layer is still conceptually central but empirically forthcoming: your topic-lock and decision documents still describe the first baseline decomposition table or figure as the next step, not as a completed France result.     

### 6. What can be credibly claimed in the abstract right now

You can credibly claim that the paper is a single-country structural labor-supply project implemented on French microdata combined with EUROMOD input files. You can also credibly say that labor supply is modeled as choice among latent job packages in a RURO-style framework; that the empirical design aims to treat opportunities consistently in estimation, welfare evaluation, and decomposition; and that microsimulation is used to map job packages into disposable income rather than to estimate behavior. Those claims are well grounded in the France pipeline and in the locked JMP framing.   

You can also credibly make the main question explicit in the abstract: whether standard structural labor-supply models over-attribute heterogeneity to preferences when unequal job opportunities are left implicit. It is also safe to keep decomposition central and to say that the paper’s contribution is the integration of a latent-jobs structural model with welfare measurement and decomposition under a responsibility-sensitive interpretation. What is important is that these claims remain about the paper’s empirical design and contribution logic, not about numerical findings already established for France.   

### 7. What should NOT be claimed yet

You should not claim that the France estimates already deliver a stable and credible decomposition of welfare inequality into opportunity and preference components. You should not claim that the France RURO estimation is fully stabilized, that preferences and opportunities are cleanly separated, or that the opportunity share is already shown to be quantitatively large. You should also not claim that the ability-set module provides a clean empirical measure of ability rather than a mixture of ability and market availability.  

You should also stop using the Belgium-style writing prototype. Do not describe the paper as a Belgium EU-SILC application, a prime-age childless single-woman model, or a 16-alternative grid unless you are explicitly discussing an obsolete fallback memo. Do not present AEI as irrevocably final in the abstract. Do not let the paper drift toward country ranking or toward the separate axiomatic theory paper. The safe line is empirical structural estimation plus welfare/decomposition, not theory, not ranking, and not settled France results.    

### 8. Recommended baseline empirical prototype for writing purposes

The recommended baseline writing prototype is: **France 2016, SRCV / EUROMOD-input cross-section, household/couples sample, RURO estimation of joint job-package choice with sampled latent alternatives, disposable income computed via EUROMOD, and welfare-inequality decomposition presented as the next empirical layer rather than as an already-final result.** This is the cleanest anchor because it matches the real France pipeline, matches the most concretely documented France sample, and avoids importing the obsolete Belgium fallback into new writing.   

For abstract and extended-abstract writing, I would therefore describe job packages only generically as discrete wage-hours opportunities, not with the old 16-alternative language. The actual implemented code uses a richer sampled universe built from wage vigintiles, 4-hour bins, and potentially occupation or industry layers, so overly narrow prototype wording would misdescribe the France implementation. In the writing, keep Germany in the background as a benchmark and diagnostics source, but make France the explicit empirical application.
