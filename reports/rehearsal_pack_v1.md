## Main-matter script (19 frames, sections as printed on the first slide of each)

**1 — Title.** *(unnumbered, `[plain]`)*
On slide: "Unequal Job Opportunities and the Measurement of Welfare Inequality" · Hisham Haydar · LISER and University of Luxembourg · France, EU-SILC priced through EUROMOD. · "Seminar of 17 September. Welfare content is presented under ruling R6; the quantitative decomposition is work in progress."
Say: Thank you. The question is how much of the inequality in money-metric well-being is attributable to unequal job opportunities rather than to differences in taste, and what happens to that attribution when a model leaves opportunities out. I will show the model, the estimates, the fit, the welfare definition and a descriptive welfare baseline. The quantitative decomposition is not ready and I will say exactly where it stands.

**2 — "Two people with the same tastes and the same wage need not face the same set of jobs."** *(2/19 — section: Question and contribution)*
On slide: two questions — how much of measured welfare inequality is attributable to unequal job access and earning opportunities, rather than to preferences; what does a model that assumes a common choice set do to that attribution. Caveat: "Both questions are about a measure, not about a policy counterfactual."
Say: The two questions are the same two questions as in every version of this paper; nothing in the recent rulings touched them. The first is an attribution question about a money-metric inequality index. The second is a methodological question: a discrete-choice labour supply model that gives every household the same alternatives has to explain non-employment with a taste parameter, and I want to know whether that relabelling survives into the welfare accounting.

**3 — "The contribution is a structural opportunity set plus a welfare measure defined on it."** *(3/19)*
On slide: the RURO model; the Haydar–Maniquet $W^1$ measure on the estimated reachable set; a decomposition architecture separating preferences, job access, earning opportunities and household endowments and needs. Caveat: "Third item: the architecture is presented today. Its quantitative output is not."
Say: The contribution has three parts and today only the first two carry numbers. The model estimates the opportunity distribution jointly with preferences. The welfare measure is taken from the companion theory paper with François Maniquet. The decomposition is the part under revision: the operators are settled, the estimand for the counterfactual attained bundle is not, and I will come back to that at the end.

**4 — "France, EU-SILC priced through EUROMOD: 1,540 single-adult and 2,223 couple households."** *(4/19 — section: Data and the structural RURO model)*
On slide: estimation frames (corrected criterion-A panels, S11 model of record); observed job plus 100 drawn alternatives, all priced through the tax-benefit system; fit diagnostics use 2,048 common quadrature nodes per household; weights are `dwt`. Caveat: "S11 is the single model of record on these slides. Earlier S8 and R240 frames and their estimates are not shown and are not comparable."
Say: Two estimation samples, singles and couples, on the corrected criterion-A frames. Everything on the slides after this point is S11. I am deliberately not showing the older S8 or R240 numbers alongside, because the frames differ and a side-by-side would invite a comparison the samples do not support. Each household contributes its observed job and a hundred drawn alternatives, each of which is run through EUROMOD so the consumption argument is a real disposable income, not an approximation. The second number on the slide is a different object: the positive-fit diagnostics integrate over a common panel of two thousand and forty-eight priced quadrature nodes per household, not over the estimation draws.

**5 — "A job is a package; a household draws from its own distribution over packages."** *(5/19)*
On slide: TikZ diagram — access/employment mass, hours (piecewise, 35-hour peak), occupation (sex-specific), wage offer (log-normal) feeding an opportunity density $g_i(\cdot)$; preferences $u_i(c,\ell)$ separately; observed job = argmax over the drawn set. Caveat: "Access and capability are not separately identified; the access factor is their product."
Say: This is the architecture. A job is not an hours cell: it is hours, occupation and a wage, and whether the household can reach it at all. The opportunity density factorises into an access margin, a piecewise-constant hours factor with a peak at the statutory thirty-five hour week, sex-specific occupation availability, and an occupation-conditioned log-normal wage offer. Preferences enter separately. The observed job is the argmax over what was drawn. The identification caveat is on the slide and I keep it there all talk: the access factor mixes personal capability and market availability and the cross-section does not separate them.

**6 — "Preferences and the opportunity density are estimated in one likelihood."** *(6/19)*
On slide: $\Pr(j_i\mid x_i)\propto\exp\{u_i(c_{ij},\ell_{ij})+\log g_i(j)-\log q_i(j)\}$, with $u$/$g$/$q$ glossed. Caveat: "The proposal correction is what makes the drawn alternatives a simulator for the estimated set rather than the choice set itself."
Say: One likelihood, two structural objects. The proposal density is a numerical device: the alternatives are drawn from it and it is divided back out, so it is not part of the economics. That separation matters later, because the welfare measure I am going to define must not depend on the proposal density, and I will show that it does not. Estimating preferences and opportunities jointly is what stops a taste parameter from quietly absorbing an availability parameter.

**7 — "S11 model of record: 41 free parameters for singles, 47 for couples."** *(7/19 — section: Positive estimates and fit)*
On slide: table — free parameters 41/47, estimation households 1,540/2,223, objective at $\hat\theta$ 6253.463/10283.034. Caveat: "Objectives reproduce exactly under the independent diagnostics evaluator, package commit 55bb0d0."

**9 — "Calibration conditioned on prediction: observed hours against predicted hours, by predicted decile."** *(9/19)*

**10 — "The access kernel is estimated, and it varies across households."** *(10/19 — section: Evidence for heterogeneous opportunities)*
On slide: figure `kernelacc_r6` — coefficient plot with 95% CI bars, "S11 singles, log access index." Caveat: "S11 singles; horizontal bars are 1.96 × CR1 cluster-robust standard errors. Structural and model-conditional; not causal."
Say: This is the access block of the estimated opportunity density. The point is not any individual coefficient, it is that the block is estimated rather than imposed and that it moves: households in different regional access environments, and in different survey strata, face materially different employment masses at the same preferences and the same wage. This is the evidence that the opportunity side of the model is doing work. It is also the evidence the welfare measure later rests on, which is why I show it before the measure and not after.

**11 — "So is the earning-opportunity kernel: an offer distribution, not a single wage."** *(11/19)*
On slide: figure `kernelwage_r6` — coefficient plot with 95% CI bars, "S11 singles, log wage-offer density." Caveat: "S11 singles; occupation terms shift the offer location. Dispersion $\hat\sigma=0.382$ ($z=28.7$)."
Say: The wage side is an offer density with an estimated dispersion, not a point wage attached to each person. Education and experience shift the location, occupation shifts it again, and the residual dispersion is precisely estimated. Two households with identical observed wages can have different earning opportunities in this model, because what they face is a distribution. That is the second half of what I mean by unequal job opportunities, and together with the access block it is what the welfare measure has to be defined on.

**12 — "Haydar–Maniquet $W^1$: the consumption at home that would leave the household exactly as well off."** *(12/19 — section: The welfare measure)*
On slide: $W^1_i=m_i(o;z_i)$ where $u_i(m_i(o),o)=u_i(z_i)$. Caveat: "Mapping F is the primary empirical implementation (ruling R1). Mapping M is sensitivity only and requires an unidentified intensity parameter; it is not in the baseline."
Say: Q&A note — ruling R1, `JMP_W1_fork_ruling_v1.md` Appendix A. If asked why this reference and not the market-job reference: the fork analysis compared Mapping F, the full feasible set including non-employment, against Mapping M, the market-job universe. R1 rules F primary for the current paper. M at its dense-law endpoint stops being a household-specific object and becomes a whole-market reference, and at finite intensity it needs a set-size parameter the data do not identify. That is recorded as a limitation and as future work, not as a competing headline. I am not claiming F is the right reference in the theory; I am claiming it is the one this empirical specification identifies.

**13 — "Under the current specification $W^1$ coincides with the staying-home equivalent — as a fact about this domain."** *(13/19)*
On slide: $W^1_{i,F}=C_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\exp[\tfrac{L_i(j_i^{\mathrm{obs}})-L_i(o)}{\beta_c}]$; inputs are $C_i^{\mathrm{obs}}, L_i(j^{\mathrm{obs}}), L_i(o), \beta_c$ — nothing else; no opportunity density $g$, no proposal $q$, no shock $\varepsilon$, no intensity $\kappa$. Caveat: "This is an empirical-domain coincidence under log consumption and universally available non-employment. It is not the theorem $W^1=W^4$ in the Haydar–Maniquet theory."
Say: Q&A note — ruling R1 and the BASELINE-F-1 ruling, section 2. The most likely question here is whether I have just proved that measure one and measure four are the same thing. I have not, and the ruling is explicit about it. The coincidence has two ingredients, both specific to what is estimated: consumption enters utility in logs, and non-employment is behaviourally available to every household. Change either and the two measures separate. If asked about the consumption argument: ruling E2 confirms that $C^{\mathrm{obs}}$ must be the exact object entering the estimated utility at the observed state, not a re-chosen normative income concept, and the measure-correspondence audit closed that provenance before the baseline was allowed to run.

**17 — "The open problem: the baseline is evaluated at the observed bundle, but an operator moves it."** *(17/19)*
On slide: TikZ flow — coalition $S$ → counterfactual environment → attained bundle $z_i^S$ → $W^1_F(z_i^S)$ → inequality $I_S$, annotated "this step is the unresolved one." Say: Q&A note — ruling R5. Here is the honest statement of where the paper is stuck. The welfare measure is evaluated at the bundle the household actually attained. Under a counterfactual that equalises access, the household would attain a different bundle, and the measure needs that bundle. Two routes are on the table. One conditions the behavioural latent state on the observed choice and carries it across counterfactual worlds, which raises the question of whether the max-stable construction supplies a legitimate cross-world coupling and whether it needs the unidentified intensity parameter. The other integrates the measure over the model-implied counterfactual choice distribution, which is cleaner but answers a different question: the inequality of expected welfare is not the expected inequality of welfare, and those two are not interchangeable. The design document has to pick one and defend it, and until it does there are no shares.

## Extended Q&A (beyond the deck's own slide notes)

### Q — Is there ANY preliminary read on which factor matters more, even before the final decomposition architecture is ready?

This answer is about a **separate, bounded, preliminary exercise (DECOMP-2, under the DECOMP-PRESEMINAR-1 ruling)** — it is not the P/A/B/D architecture on slide 16, and it does not walk back anything said on slides 16–18. Use it only if a discussant explicitly asks for a preliminary read after hearing slide 18; do not volunteer it as part of the main talk.

Say: Separately from the four-operator architecture I've just shown you, and outside it, a bounded three-factor exercise has been run on the current, correct $W^1_F$ measure — the same measure on the baseline slides you've just seen, not the withdrawn ex-ante functional. It splits **P** (systematic utility heterogeneity), **A** (local labour-market access — region, urban/rural, year) and **B** (earning opportunities), with household resources, needs and composition held fixed rather than decomposed. This is explicitly a preliminary, model-based side-analysis, not the final architecture.

Qualitatively, and I'll give you the shape only, no numbers: earning opportunities dominate local labour-market access — in every sample and every reporting scale I've checked, not just on average. I'm not going to give you a number for preferences, because its sign flips between the unequivalised and the equivalised reporting in both samples, so no directional claim about it is defensible yet.

And the movable share here is small, which is by design, not because opportunities are unimportant: household resources, needs and composition are held fixed in every counterfactual in this exercise, and that fixed piece is most of what drives the dispersion in the money metric to begin with. So a small movable share is a property of what this bounded exercise chose to hold fixed, not a finding that opportunities don't matter.

### Q — Why fix the time endowment at eighty hours? Does that choice matter?

Say: Leisure is defined as eighty hours a week minus hours worked — that's a convention, and the model is not indifferent to it. A diagnostic re-estimation at seventy-five and ninety hours, holding everything else to the certified protocol, shows the fit moves materially in both directions, and at ninety a second leisure-curvature coefficient joins the boundary that is interior at eighty — that's a genuine change in what the model can identify, not just a relabelling of the same optimum. A separate check on the leisure-scale normalizer, by contrast, found a pure units artefact that moves nothing. So eighty hours is a maintained convention, not a free choice the model is agnostic to, and it's on the list of open items rather than settled. I'll note this particular sensitivity check is a completed diagnostic on a branch not yet merged into the main estimation line, so take the direction of the finding as solid and the exact magnitudes as provisional.
