Novelty Audit of Structural Decomposition of Money Metric Well Being Inequality
Economics job market paper literature ruling and positioning memo
Bottom line
The exact integration is plausibly novel, but none of its broad ingredients is. The defensible claim is the first known use of a RURO structural labour supply model to allocate inequality in an opportunity-sensitive money-metric welfare distribution across preference, access, earning-opportunity, and household-resource operators through all-coalition welfare recomputation and a grouped Shapley-Owen-Shorrocks allocation. The paper should not claim a new Shapley method, the first structural labour-supply inequality decomposition, the first decomposition of money-metric welfare inequality, or the first welfare analysis that distinguishes preferences from opportunities.13
Literature searched through 9 September 2026
 
Executive rulings
The novelty question is best answered as a conjunction test. Several literatures supply close pieces of the design. The relevant question is whether a prior paper contains the same outcome, welfare metric, structural model, factor system, coalition algorithm, and complete recomputation rule in one empirical exercise.
Ruling	Finding
A	Inherited. The Shapley allocation, Shorrocks extension to distributional statistics, Owen grouping, and the main zero-versus-equalized and nesting conventions are established methods. The paper can adapt and implement them, but should not present the mathematical allocation rule as new.
B	Qualified novelty. The generic move from counterfactual coalitions to a characteristic function is inherited. The economically coherent P, A, B, and D operators inside the estimated RURO model appear to be a new game specification if the paper formally defines how each operator changes feasible jobs, wages, preferences, resources and needs while keeping every coalition admissible.
C	Plausibly novel integration. No located paper combines household-specific RURO preferences and latent job opportunities, an opportunity-sensitive money-metric welfare distribution, separate P/A/B/D equalization, full welfare and inequality recomputation for every coalition, and grouped Shapley-Owen-Shorrocks attribution.
D	Use a narrow first-known claim. Name the model, welfare object, four operator classes, all-coalition recomputation, and grouped allocation in the same sentence. A conservative introduce-and-implement formulation is even safer.
E	Avoid broad first claims, causal language for observational shares, and claims of a unique or baseline-free decomposition. State that the shares are model- and operator-dependent allocations of interactions, conditional on identification, reference welfare choices, and the counterfactual equalization rules.

Confidence in ruling C is moderate rather than absolute. The combination survived targeted searches across Shapley inequality decomposition, behavioural microsimulation, random utility labour supply, RURO job-choice welfare, equivalent-income analysis, fairness and equality-of-opportunity measurement. Novelty cannot be proved by search alone, and the claim should remain qualified by to our knowledge.
The exact novelty object
The proposed contribution has six linked elements. Removing any one of them moves the claim into a literature with direct precedents.
•	Outcome: an inequality statistic computed over a household distribution of opportunity-sensitive money-metric well-being, rather than disposable income, earnings, labour supply, compensating variation from one reform, or aggregate social welfare.
•	Structural information: estimated heterogeneous preferences and household-specific latent job opportunities in a random-utility random-opportunity labour-supply model.
•	Factors: P for preferences; A for access to employment, hours, and occupations; B for earning or wage opportunities; and D for household resources and needs.
•	Operators: each factor is equalized through a model-consistent structural counterfactual, not replaced by a fitted statistical component or a period-specific coefficient vector without an economic feasibility rule.
•	Recomputation: choices, household welfare, the full welfare distribution, and the inequality statistic are recomputed for every required coalition.
•	Allocation: interactions across factor groups and lower-level components are assigned by a grouped Shapley-Owen-Shorrocks rule.
The novelty claim should attach to this full conjunction. A claim attached only to the final allocation formula is not defensible because Shapley, Owen, Shorrocks, Sastre-Trannoy, Chantreuil-Trannoy, and Audoly et al. already supply the relevant mathematical machinery.123
Ruling A Mathematical decomposition
Ruling A is yes: the mathematical allocation is inherited. Shorrocks shows how to map a distributional statistic into a cooperative game and average marginal contributions over factor orderings. Owen supplies values for games with a priori unions, which permits grouping. Sastre and Trannoy analyze implementation choices such as zero-income versus equalized-income baselines and nested Shapley versus Owen. Chantreuil and Trannoy compare efficiency and marginality properties and identify a role for Owen's value in inequality decomposition.
Audoly, McGee, Ocampo, and Paz-Pardo make the implication for modern structural work explicit. Their framework treats a nonlinear outcome as a function of inputs, evaluates all submodels or coalitions, and uses Shapley-Owen-Shorrocks contributions for inputs and groups. The paper at issue can cite this as the implementation template. Its methodological contribution is therefore not a new allocation axiom or a new closed-form Shapley formula.
What can still be methodological
A methodological contribution remains possible if the paper shows that the P/A/B/D counterfactuals form a coherent structural game. The useful formal content would include:
•	a precise map from each coalition to the household choice set, budget set, preferences, wage schedule, resources and needs;
•	proof or transparent computation showing that every coalition defines an admissible RURO problem;
•	a clear normalization for equalization and a fixed reference for money-metric comparisons;
•	rules for interactions within and across groups, including the exact Owen nesting convention;
•	a computational identity showing that the reported shares add to the chosen inequality gap up to simulation error.
Recommended characterization: an application-specific structural operator construction and implementation, not a new decomposition principle.
Ruling B Structural game and operators
Ruling B is partly yes. The generic characteristic-function construction is not new: many Shapley decompositions define the value of a coalition as the statistic generated when those factors are active and the rest are set to a reference. Likewise, counterfactual microsimulation papers swap tax systems, wage structures, populations, preferences, or restrictions and recompute the outcome.
The specific P/A/B/D construction appears new enough to claim. Its novelty comes from maintaining a common money-metric welfare object while separately equalizing tastes, job access, earnings opportunities, and household resources or needs in a model where those inputs jointly determine both choice and welfare. The distinction between A and B is especially important: prior work often bundles labour-demand restrictions with employment probabilities or treats potential wages as a conventional income-generation parameter.
The operator contribution will be credible only if the paper explains what equalization means. Replacing parameters by their mean, replacing distributions by a common reference distribution, rank-preserving transport, and resampling from a pooled distribution create different games. The paper should describe how household heterogeneity, correlation across factors, and equilibrium or adding-up constraints are handled. Otherwise a referee can characterize P/A/B/D as labels on standard counterfactual swaps.
Ruling C Novelty of the full integration
Ruling C is plausibly yes. The closest literature forms three bridges, but no located paper closes the triangle.
Bridge one Structural Shapley decompositions of income inequality
Mühlhan 2023 is the strongest challenge. It estimates a discrete-choice labour-supply model augmented by a separate restriction equation for involuntary unemployment. It decomposes the 2004-2015 change in German disposable-income inequality into direct and indirect policy effects, direct and indirect wage effects, preferences, restrictions, population, and nominal growth. It evaluates all orderings, simulates 128 counterfactual scenarios, and reports the Shorrocks-Shapley average across 5,040 seven-factor decompositions. This is already a structural labour-supply Shapley inequality decomposition with separate preference and access-like channels.5
The remaining difference is substantive. Mühlhan's outcome is pre-tax or disposable household income. The paper does not construct an opportunity-sensitive money-metric welfare level for each household and then recompute that welfare distribution under P/A/B/D equalization. Its restriction model is a binary involuntary-unemployment hurdle rather than a household-specific distribution of latent jobs over employment, hours, and occupation. Its factors describe changes between two dates rather than a within-distribution equalization exercise.
Bargain 2012 and Jessen 2019 establish the broader structural precedent. They combine behavioural labour-supply microsimulation, counterfactual income distributions, inequality or poverty statistics, and Shapley-style averaging. Bargain isolates direct policy, induced labour-supply, and residual environmental changes. Jessen attributes German inequality changes to policy, wages, population, and induced behavioural responses. Their outcome remains disposable income, and neither constructs the four-part opportunity-sensitive welfare game.
Bridge two Decomposition of money metric welfare inequality
Creedy and Hérault 2011 blocks any broad claim to the first decomposition of money-metric welfare inequality. Using a structural discrete-hours random-utility labour-supply model, they construct a distribution of money-metric utility, calculate an Atkinson index and social welfare, and decompose changes between Australian tax and population states. Their money-metric exercise fully calculates the relevant welfare distribution for four policy-population states and averages the two possible paths, which is the two-factor Shapley value.4
The difference is again decisive. Creedy and Hérault use tax structure and population as the two factors. They explicitly state that a separate behavioural effect cannot be isolated once money-metric utility is used in their setup. They do not estimate latent job-opportunity distributions, equalize preferences, access, wages, or needs, or use a grouped four-factor game.
Bridge three RURO welfare and fairness
Aaberge and Colombino 2013 is the closest early RURO welfare benchmark. It estimates heterogeneous preferences and job opportunities, simulates tax rules, evaluates individual welfare with income and money-metric utility, and compares equality-of-outcome with equality-of-opportunity social criteria. Jia and Thoresen 2021 derive money-metric welfare changes in a job-choice model in which the opportunity measure enters indirect utility and show how the model can compensate for differences in job opportunities. Jacquet, Jia, and Thoresen 2026 separate preferences from circumstances in the distribution of compensating variation by constructing a reference-preference welfare measure.68
These papers make the welfare and opportunity components non-novel in isolation. They do not perform all-coalition Shapley attribution of a level-inequality statistic across P/A/B/D. The same is true of recent RURO policy applications by de Mahieu, Rees, Thoresen and Vatto, Colombino and Islam, and Green and Sonnewald: they recompute labour supply and welfare for policy scenarios, but do not build the proposed decomposition game.9

Adjacent literatures and why they do not defeat the claim
Equivalent income and welfare rankings
Aaberge, Dagsvik, and Strom 1989 already compare inequality rankings based on equivalent income with rankings based on money income in estimated labour-supply models. Aaberge, Dagsvik, and Strom 1995 estimate a job-opportunity model and evaluate welfare effects of tax reforms. Decoster and Haan 2015 and Bargain et al. 2013 retrieve preference heterogeneity from structural labour-supply models to construct comparable welfare orderings. These papers rule out claims to the first structural labour-supply welfare distribution or the first use of estimated preferences in money-metric well-being.7
Recent RURO policy evaluation
The recent RURO literature is active. De Mahieu 2021 evaluates Belgian in-work benefits with labour-supply and welfare effects. Rees, Thoresen, and Vatto 2023 compare child-benefit designs and report changes in money-metric utility. Colombino and Islam 2025 search for optimal tax-transfer rules under job-loss and job-creation scenarios with comparable money-metric welfare. Green and Sonnewald 2025 evaluate marginal Belgian income-tax reforms with a RURO model and money-metric utility. These papers make the model-welfare pairing established, but none performs the proposed structural attribution.
Fairness and opportunity-sensitive welfare
Ooghe, Schokkaert, and Serruys 2025 define fairness gaps as differences between actual and fair money-metric utilities and analytically separate revenue, efficiency, and unfair inequality. Piacquadio 2026 builds a non-paternalistic deprivation criterion around opportunity sets and household needs. These contributions sharpen the normative antecedents. They do not estimate and decompose the same empirical welfare distribution.10
Inequality of opportunity
Bourguignon, Ferreira, and Menendez 2007 and Ferreira and Gignoux 2011 measure the share of inequality in earnings, income, or consumption associated with observed circumstances. Azevedo, Sanfelice, and Nguyen 2012 apply Shapley logic to changes in a welfare aggregate, meaning an income or consumption aggregate rather than utility. The later IOp literature often uses Shapley values to apportion fitted opportunity inequality across circumstances.11
This literature differs at three levels. Its outcome is typically earnings, household income, consumption, or a predicted circumstance component; its opportunity concept is based on observed ex ante circumstances rather than an estimated choice set; and its counterfactuals generally do not solve the household's structural choice problem and recompute money-metric welfare for every coalition.
Preferences versus productivity
Bergstrom and Dodds 2020 use labour-supply elasticities to infer how productivity and preferences contribute to earnings inequality and construct counterfactual incomes that equalize each element. This is a useful conceptual comparator because it treats preferences as a source of observed inequality. It has no Shapley allocation, latent job opportunities, household money metric, or access-resource split.12
Structural counterfactual income decompositions
Bourguignon, Ferreira, and Leite 2008 import wage, occupational-choice, endowment, demographic, and non-labour-income structures across countries to create counterfactual income distributions. This is a direct precedent for recomputing nonlinear distributional statistics under structural factor swaps. The paper notes the path-dependence problem but reports selected orderings rather than the complete grouped allocation proposed here.
Ruling D Safe novelty language
Recommended abstract sentence
To our knowledge, this paper is the first to use a random-utility random-opportunity structural labour-supply model to attribute inequality in an opportunity-sensitive money-metric welfare distribution to preferences, access to employment, hours and occupations, earning opportunities, and household resources and needs by fully recomputing welfare under all coalition counterfactuals and applying a grouped Shapley-Owen-Shorrocks allocation.14
More conservative sentence
We introduce and implement a structural decomposition of opportunity-sensitive money-metric welfare inequality. Using an estimated RURO labour-supply model, we equalize preferences, job access, earning opportunities, and household resources or needs in all required coalitions, recompute household welfare and inequality in each counterfactual, and allocate the resulting gap with a grouped Shapley-Owen-Shorrocks rule.
Recommendation. Use the conservative sentence in the main contribution paragraph and reserve the first-known sentence for the literature review or conclusion. If the abstract needs a first claim, keep every narrowing element. Do not shorten it to first decomposition of welfare inequality or first structural Shapley decomposition.
One sentence literature positioning
Relative to structural microsimulation decompositions of disposable-income inequality, our outcome is household money-metric welfare; relative to RURO welfare studies, our contribution is an all-coalition attribution of welfare inequality across preferences, access, earnings opportunities, and resources or needs.
Contribution paragraph for the introduction
The paper combines three previously separate strands. Shapley-Shorrocks and Owen decompositions provide an order-invariant allocation rule for nonlinear distributional statistics. Behavioural microsimulation studies use structural labour-supply models to decompose changes in disposable-income inequality. RURO welfare studies use estimated preferences and job opportunities to evaluate money-metric welfare and equality of opportunity. We join these elements by defining model-consistent equalization operators for preferences, access, earning opportunities, and household resources or needs; recomputing household welfare and its inequality for every coalition; and applying a grouped Shapley-Owen-Shorrocks allocation to the resulting structural game.
Ruling E Claims to avoid
Claim to avoid	Why it is unsafe
First Shapley decomposition of inequality	Shorrocks and a large subsequent literature already establish this.
First grouped or Owen inequality decomposition	Owen grouping and its application to inequality decomposition are established.
First Shapley decomposition of money metric welfare inequality	Creedy and Hérault provide a direct two-factor precedent.
First structural labour supply Shapley decomposition	Bargain, Jessen, and especially Mühlhan provide direct precedents for income inequality.
First RURO welfare analysis	Aaberge-Colombino, Jia-Thoresen, de Mahieu, Rees et al., Colombino-Islam, and Green-Sonnewald precede it.
First to distinguish preferences from opportunities in welfare	Aaberge-Colombino, Decoster-Haan, Jia-Thoresen, Jacquet et al., Ooghe et al., and Piacquadio are prior art.
First opportunity-sensitive welfare measure	The theoretical and empirical fairness literature is broader than this paper.
The shares are causal effects	Unless additional identification supports causal interventions, the shares are structural accounting allocations conditional on the model.
The decomposition is unique	The Shapley value is unique under its axioms for a fixed game; the game itself depends on the baseline, reference welfare metric, equalization operator and grouping.
The factors are additive sources of welfare	Shapley allocates interactions; it does not establish separability or remove normative dependence.
Access and wages are separately identified without qualification	The paper must explain which data and restrictions distinguish the numerosity or composition of job offers from wage heterogeneity.
The result is general equilibrium	Use partial-equilibrium language unless wages, job creation, fiscal balance and market clearing are solved jointly.

 
Identification and robustness conditions for the novelty claim
The paper's claim will be more persuasive if the implementation makes the following choices visible. These are not extra novelty requirements; they are the conditions under which the operator contribution can be assessed rather than treated as relabelled reweighting.
Issue	What the paper should disclose
Reference welfare metric	Fix the reference prices, reference wage or preference ordering, treatment of random utility, and household equivalence or needs scale. Show whether rankings and shares change under plausible alternatives.
Equalization target	State whether each operator sets a parameter, a predicted distribution, a rank, a latent draw, or a conditional distribution to a common reference.
Joint dependence	Explain whether equalizing one factor preserves its empirical dependence with the others. Report sensitivity to independent resampling versus conditional or rank-preserving transformations.
Access and wages	Show the exclusion restrictions or data variation that identify job availability separately from wages and preferences. Clarify whether occupation access is extensive, intensive, or compositional.
Household resources and needs	Separate cash and non-labour resources from normative needs adjustments where possible. These enter welfare differently and may warrant subgroups under D.
Choice recomputation	For every coalition, resolve the household problem. Avoid plugging counterfactual inputs into the welfare formula while retaining baseline choices unless that shortcut is theoretically justified.
Random terms	Describe whether welfare is ex ante expected, conditional on simulated draws, or integrated over taste and opportunity shocks. Use common random numbers across coalitions to reduce simulation noise.
Grouping	Report both group shares and, if used, within-group allocations. State whether the Owen value, nested Shapley, or another coalition structure is implemented.
Baseline dependence	Report at least one alternative reference household or equalization baseline. A first-known operator architecture can be novel even when numerical shares are baseline-sensitive.
Uncertainty	Bootstrap or otherwise propagate estimation and simulation uncertainty through the full decomposition. Report add-up error and Monte Carlo error.

Recommended contribution hierarchy
1.	Primary contribution: the opportunity-sensitive money-metric welfare distribution and its structural P/A/B/D counterfactual game inside RURO.
2.	Secondary contribution: full recomputation of household choice and welfare for every coalition, making interactions explicit rather than using a local or plug-in approximation.
3.	Tertiary contribution: the grouped Shapley-Owen-Shorrocks summary of that game, presented as an established allocation device adapted to the new structural object.
Why this hierarchy matters. It prevents the paper's novelty from resting on an algorithm that referees will recognize immediately. It also makes clear that the contribution survives if a referee prefers a different inequality index or requests alternative Shapley baselines: those changes affect the empirical allocation, while the new object is the structural welfare game.
Overall ruling
Defensible with narrow wording
The proposed paper is not first in any broad component category. It sits at an unoccupied intersection identified by the audit: RURO estimation of preferences and household-specific latent job opportunities; a household distribution of opportunity-sensitive money-metric welfare; separate equalization of P, A, B and D; complete coalition-level recomputation of welfare and inequality; and grouped Shapley-Owen-Shorrocks attribution. That intersection supports a qualified first-known claim, subject to the paper making its operator definitions and identification assumptions explicit.
Search method and scope
The audit followed citation chains and targeted searches across six literatures: cooperative-game inequality decomposition; behavioural tax-benefit microsimulation; structural labour supply and RURO job choice; equivalent income and money-metric utility; fairness and opportunity-sensitive welfare; and empirical inequality of opportunity. Searches combined terms for Shapley, Owen, Shorrocks, equivalent income, money-metric utility, preferences, job access, latent jobs, random opportunities, full counterfactual recomputation, labour supply, and welfare inequality. Priority was given to journal articles, working-paper versions hosted by institutions, and original methodological papers.
The ruling is a literature assessment through 9 September 2026, not a proof of universal priority. Working papers, unpublished manuscripts, papers under different terminology, and contemporaneous research can escape even a broad search. The recommended language therefore uses to our knowledge and defines the claimed intersection precisely.
