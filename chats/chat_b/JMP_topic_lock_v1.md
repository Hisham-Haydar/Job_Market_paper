# Working title

Opportunity Constraints as a Driver of Welfare Inequality: A Latent-Jobs Structural Decomposition Approach. 

# Main research question

How much observed inequality in money-metric well-being is due to unequal job opportunities rather than heterogeneous preferences, once labor supply is modeled as discrete choice over latent job packages and welfare is evaluated under constrained feasible sets? 

# Supporting subquestions

How should the opportunity mechanism be estimated separately from preferences?

How much do welfare levels change when opportunities are modeled explicitly rather than treated implicitly?

How large is the opportunity-driven share of welfare inequality under a responsibility-sensitive interpretation?

# Preferred direction

Single-country structural JMP centered on latent jobs / RURO, money-metric welfare, and decomposition of welfare inequality into opportunity and preference components. Microsimulation is used only to map job packages into disposable income. 

# Narrower fallback

Run a parsimonious prototype with the smallest defensible job-package definition: hours categories × wage or earnings categories, optionally sector, with a simple opportunity mechanism varying by region × education. Use that prototype to test whether the opportunity share is nontrivial and stable. 

# What this paper is NOT

Not a generic cross-country ranking exercise.

Not another standard tax-reform simulation paper.

Not a microsimulation-led paper.

Not the separate theory paper. 

# Main empirical prototype

Estimate a single-country structural labor-supply model for working-age individuals or households, excluding self-employed initially. Model choice over discrete job packages. Compute disposable income for each package with tax-benefit simulation. Estimate preferences plus an opportunity mechanism varying with observable circumstances. Main output: one decomposition table/figure showing baseline welfare inequality and the change under opportunity equalization, preference neutralization, and both together. 

# Main risk

Identification. Preferences and opportunities may be weakly separable, making the decomposition look mechanical. Secondary risk: normative vulnerability if the welfare metric and responsibility stance are not stated clearly. 

# Immediate next action

Implement the minimal prototype, estimate the simple opportunity mechanism, compute baseline welfare inequality, and produce one decomposition figure plus one robustness check. If the opportunity share is quantitatively meaningful and reasonably stable, lock the topic.
