As the earnings distribution widens, gains and losses become bigger (more money is redistributed), and Y becomes lower. This implies that, for given labour supply elasticities, in-work benefits will be more desirable in countries with large earnings disparities.

Labour force participation may also generate externalities. Some of these externalities take the form of fiscal externalities, where higher employment rates affect the demand for certain commodities that are initially taxed or subsidised by the government.
