# Speaking outline — seminar deck V16 (18 slides, about 40 minutes)

Deck: `beamer/build/JMP_seminar_beamer_v16.pdf`. Full spoken notes: `reports/rehearsal_pack_v16.md`. Read every number off the slide; none is repeated here.

| # | Slide | Say (one line) | Min |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | The share of well-being inequality that goes with unequal jobs depends on the welfare question you ask. | 1 |
| 2 | Same income, differently placed | Low earnings mix taste, missing opportunities and household resources; well-being Ginis differ from consumption Ginis. | 2 |
| 3 | Literature gap | Latent-jobs models, money metrics and Shapley rules all exist; nobody decomposes a *level* of well-being inequality across them. | 3 |
| 4 | Research question | Read the question and the three subquestions; stress "associated", not caused. | 2 |
| 5 | Model: utility *(eq. 1)* | A job's value is its leisure plus log consumption; the split is what later turns utility into euros. | 2 |
| 6 | Model: choice law *(eq. 2)* | A job is chosen because it is valued or plentiful; household-specific availability is what separates the two. | 3 |
| 7 | Matched households | Two men, same tastes: one has far more jobs, the other better-paid ones — neither is better placed on every margin. | 2 |
| 8 | Subquestion 1 | Remove household-specific opportunities and fit worsens; not a formal test. | 2 |
| 9 | Theory figure | Give every job the same pay and ask what is as good as the actual situation; the choice of reference is the welfare question. | 2 |
| 10 | EA *(eq. 4)* | EA prices the whole prospect: flat pay that makes the household's own opportunity environment equally valuable. Shown first for this talk, not ranked first. | 3 |
| 11 | ATT *(eq. 3)* | ATT prices the job actually held against staying home; opportunities matter only through that job. | 2 |
| 12 | Two different questions | Outcomes versus prospects; neither corrects the other; neither is designated primary. | 2 |
| 13 | Decomposition *(eq. 5)* | Equalise preferences, local access and wage-offer location; resources, needs and composition held fixed; Shapley averages over orders. | 3 |
| 14 | **Central result** | Singles: earnings lead under ATT, access leads under EA; couples do not reverse. Mechanism: the route by which opportunities enter each measure. | 4 |
| 15 | EA results | State the range; access about three times earnings for single adults; equivalisation changes the couples result. | 2 |
| 16 | ATT results | State the range; earnings lead throughout; preference sign not robust; still preliminary. | 2 |
| 17 | What these are not | Preliminary; not causal; no parameter uncertainty; preferences are not responsibility; narrow access, fixed resources. | 2 |
| 18 | Conclusion | Three answers; next: resources and needs as a fourth channel, parameter uncertainty, finish validation. | 1 |

**Hold the line in Q&A.** EA is not the paper's primary measure — the choice between perspectives is open. Access means *local* access, not all opportunity. No share here is causal or a total share of inequality due to job opportunities.
