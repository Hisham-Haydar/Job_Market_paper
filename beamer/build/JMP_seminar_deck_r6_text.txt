Question and contribution
Two people with the same tastes and the same wage need not face the same set of jobs.




      How much of measured welfare inequality is attributable to unequal job access and earning
       opportunities, rather than to preferences?

      What does a model that assumes a common choice set do to that attribution?




   Both questions are about a measure, not about a policy counterfactual.




                                                                                                   2/19
The contribution is a structural opportunity set plus a welfare measure defined on it.




      A random-utility, random-opportunity (RURO) model in which which jobs a household can
       reach is estimated, not assumed.
      The Haydar–Maniquet money-metric measure W 1 , applied on the estimated reachable set
       rather than on a common grid.
      A decomposition architecture that separates preferences from job access, earning
       opportunities and household endowments and needs.


   Third item: the architecture is presented today. Its quantitative output is not.

The welfare measure
Haydar–Maniquet W 1 : the consumption at home that would leave the household exactly
as well off.




                                                                                
                 Wi1 = mi (o; zi ) where ui mi (o), o                               = ui (zi )


   zi attained bundle   o the non-employment state    mi the money metric



   Mapping F is the primary empirical implementation (ruling R1). Mapping M is sensitivity only and requires an
   unidentified intensity parameter; it is not in the baseline.



                                                                                                                  12/19
Under the current specification W 1 coincides with the staying-home equivalent — as a
fact about this domain.



                                                             h                  i
                                  1                      L (j obs )−L (o)
                                Wi,F      =    Ciobs exp i i βc i



      Inputs: Ciobs , Li (j obs ), Li (o), βc — nothing else.
      No opportunity density g , no proposal q, no shock ε, no intensity κ.


   This is an empirical-domain coincidence under log consumption and universally available non-employment. It is
   not the theorem W 1 = W 4 in the Haydar–Maniquet theory.

Decomposition architecture
Four operators, one at a time: systematic utility heterogeneity, job access, earning
opportunities, household endowments and needs.




               operator   replaces, for every household
               P          its systematic utility heterogeneity (tastes + reduced-form time constraints) by the common reference
               A          its job access by the common reference access
               B          its earning opportunities by the common reference offer distribution
               D          its household endowments and needs by the common reference budget




   Operators only. No share, no magnitude, no ordering is claimed on this slide or anywhere in this deck. Final
   decomposition semantics are work in progress.
