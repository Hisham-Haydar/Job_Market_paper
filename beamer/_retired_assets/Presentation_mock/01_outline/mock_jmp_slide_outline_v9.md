## Main deck

### Slide 1 — Opportunity constraints may be hiding inside “preference heterogeneity”

**Bullet points**

* Research question: how much of observed inequality in money-metric well-being is due to **unequal job opportunities** rather than **heterogeneous preferences**?
* Labor supply is modeled as choice among **latent job packages**, not free choice on a common menu.
* The paper is about **decomposition**, not country ranking.
* Belgium is the baseline application; Europe is extension only.

**What visual goes on the slide**

* One clean title visual: **latent jobs and opportunities → welfare measurement → inequality decomposition**.

**Main takeaway**

* The paper asks whether welfare inequality is partly being misread as taste heterogeneity because opportunities are not modeled explicitly.

### Slide 2 — In one slide: model, welfare, decomposition, result

**Bullet points**

* Estimate a structural labor-supply model with **preferences + latent opportunities**.
* Compute **opportunity-sensitive money-metric welfare**.
* Decompose welfare inequality into **opportunity** and **preference** components.
* Headline result: opportunities explain **0.039 Gini points**, or **43.8% of the explained component**.

**What visual goes on the slide**

* Three-column “paper in a nutshell” visual: **Model / Welfare / Result**, with a small decomposition figure thumbnail.

**Main takeaway**

* Once feasible job sets are modeled, unequal opportunities explain a quantitatively large share of welfare inequality. 

### Slide 3 — Economists should care because outcomes do not reveal the same thing for everyone

**Bullet points**

* The same observed bundle can be a **chosen optimum** for one person and a **constrained second-best** for another.
* If opportunities are omitted, standard models risk absorbing them into **tastes**.
* Then both the **positive interpretation** and the **normative interpretation** of inequality are distorted.
* This matters for how economists think about **compensation**, **responsibility**, and **redistribution**.

**What visual goes on the slide**

* Two-person schematic: same observed hours-income point, different feasible job sets.

**Main takeaway**

* Opportunity constraints matter because they change both the science of labor-supply heterogeneity and the welfare meaning of observed inequality.

### Slide 4 — This paper sits at the intersection of four literatures

**Bullet points**

* **Latent jobs / RURO labor supply**: Aaberge et al. (1995), Dagsvik et al. (2014), Dagsvik & Jia (2016), Capéau et al. (2016).
* **Money-metric welfare in discrete choice**: Dagsvik & Karlström (2005), Bhattacharya (2015), Bargain et al. (2013).
* **Equality of opportunity / fairness**: Roemer & Trannoy (2016), Ferreira & Gignoux (2011), Fleurbaey & Maniquet (2006).
* **Order-invariant decomposition**: Shorrocks (2013), with Audoly et al. (2025) as implementation guidance.

**What visual goes on the slide**

* Four-box literature map with named papers in each box and **This paper** in the center.

**Main takeaway**

* The unusual feature of the paper is not structural labor supply alone, but treating the estimated opportunity mechanism as a first-class distributive primitive.

### Slide 5 — Existing papers stop too early; I connect behavior, welfare, and fairness

**Bullet points**

* Constrained labor-supply papers usually stop at **fit, elasticities, or reform simulation**.
* Welfare papers with heterogeneous preferences usually stop before **explicit opportunity modeling**.
* Equality-of-opportunity papers usually work with **reduced-form circumstance partitions**, not estimated job opportunities.
* I estimate **opportunities**, translate them into **welfare**, and then **decompose welfare inequality**.

**What visual goes on the slide**

* Two-column matrix labeled **What they do** / **What I do**.

**Main takeaway**

* The contribution is integration: a latent-jobs labor-supply model, an opportunity-sensitive welfare metric, and a structural decomposition of welfare inequality in one design.

### Slide 6 — Belgium is the clean first application

**Bullet points**

* Data: **Belgian EU-SILC + EUROMOD** baseline tax-benefit rules.
* Sample: **3,284** childless single women aged **25–54**.
* Labor supply over **16 job packages**: non-employment + **5 hours bins × 3 wage terciles**.
* Opportunity heterogeneity: **region × education**; preference heterogeneity: **age, age², two latent taste types**.

**What visual goes on the slide**

* Belgium map plus a compact design table and a very small 16-package grid.

**Main takeaway**

* The Belgium baseline is narrow by design so the opportunity-versus-preference question is identified and interpretable in a clean first-pass setting. 

### Slide 7 — Unequal opportunities explain a large share of welfare inequality

**Bullet points**

* Disposable-income Gini: **0.164**; opportunity-sensitive welfare Gini: **0.186**.
* Opportunity equalization lowers the welfare Gini to **0.151**.
* Preference equalization lowers it to **0.140**; equalizing both lowers it to **0.097**.
* Opportunity heterogeneity explains **21.0%** of baseline welfare inequality.

**What visual goes on the slide**

* Main stacked-bar decomposition figure, with a tiny inset table showing baseline, opportunity-equalized, preference-equalized, and both-equalized Ginis.

**Main takeaway**

* Opportunity heterogeneity is quantitatively important — not dominant over preferences, but far too large to be treated as background friction.

**Bullet points**

**Main takeaway**

### Slide 10 — Identification is simple to state: ranking versus access

**Bullet points**

* The identification challenge is to separate **how people rank packages** from **which packages are available**.
* Region × education disciplines the opportunity block.
* Age and latent taste types discipline the preference block.
* Welfare is then computed **conditional on the feasible set**, not on a universal menu.

**What visual goes on the slide**

* One very simple flow chart: **Estimate model → compute welfare → equalize opportunities/preferences → decompose Gini**.

**Main takeaway**

* The paper’s structure is disciplined because the same model that separates ranking from access also generates the welfare object being decomposed.

### Slide 11 — Preference heterogeneity is meaningful, but not a catch-all residual

* Two latent taste types; the more leisure-oriented type makes up **39.0%** of the sample.

**What visual goes on the slide**

**Main takeaway**

**Main takeaway**
