# Presentation-oriented literature architecture for an opportunity-sensitive welfare inequality decomposition paper

## Main field positioning

Your paper should be positioned as a **four-way synthesis** in which the *unusual* element is not the use of a structural labor-supply model per se, but the **decision to treat the estimated opportunity mechanism as a first-class distributive primitive** and then **attribute welfare inequality to it** (rather than treating opportunity terms as a fit device). This maps cleanly to your current backbone: (i) a latent-jobs / RURO job-choice model, (ii) an opportunity-conditioned money-metric welfare level (equivalent monthly disposable income at a reference state), and (iii) a two-factor Shapley–Shorrocks decomposition into “opportunity” and “preference” components. fileciteturn6file12 fileciteturn3file6 fileciteturn6file16

The four strands below are the **committee-facing** way to say “I belong to the field” while keeping the talk tightly about *your* object (decomposition of welfare inequality) rather than drifting into a survey.

**Constrained-choice structural labor supply with latent opportunities (RURO / latent jobs / restricted offers).** Your baseline design is recognizably in the Scandinavian “job packages + random opportunities” tradition: discrete job packages, explicit separation of preference and opportunity blocks, and an opportunity model that varies systematically by circumstances (here: region × education in entity["country","Belgium","country in western europe"]). fileciteturn6file12 fileciteturn3file6

**Money-metric welfare measurement built on discrete-choice structure (EV/CV/equivalent-income logic).** You are not just estimating behavioral parameters: you translate the estimated constrained-choice environment into an individual welfare level measured as **equivalent monthly disposable income at a reference (non-employment/full-leisure) state**, i.e., a money metric that is interpretable in policy space. fileciteturn6file12 fileciteturn3file6

**Equality of opportunity and responsibility-sensitive welfare analysis (circumstances vs responsibility-relevant heterogeneity).** You explicitly invite the normative distinction that committees recognize from equality-of-opportunity and fairness-in-taxation work: which heterogeneities are “compensable” (opportunities/circumstances) versus “responsibility-relevant” (often tastes/effort), and you present sensitivity of the opportunity component to alternative responsibility stances. fileciteturn3file12 fileciteturn6file16

**Order-invariant inequality decomposition as a scientific object (Shapley–Shorrocks attribution delivered by structural counterfactuals).** Your contribution is not “another welfare index”; it is a **structural decomposition architecture**: define counterfactual equalizations (opportunities, preferences), compute welfare inequality under each, and attribute the explained component using an order-independent Shapley–Shorrocks rule. fileciteturn6file16 fileciteturn6file12

## Why economists should care

The economic problem is that **observed labor-supply outcomes (employment, hours, wages) are not pure preference revelation** when job opportunities differ across individuals: the same observed hours–income point can be a chosen optimum for one worker and a constrained second-best for another because their feasible job packages differ. fileciteturn3file6 fileciteturn3file0 This matters because standard structural labor-supply estimation and policy simulation—especially when combined with tax-benefit calculators—are routinely used to produce welfare conclusions, but without an explicit opportunity block those conclusions risk absorbing opportunity inequality into “tastes,” contaminating both the positive object (preference heterogeneity) and the normative object (who is truly worse off). fileciteturn3file18 fileciteturn6file16

The welfare-analysis and policy relevance is that **distributional evaluation depends on whether inequality reflects constrained access or chosen trade-offs**: if a large share of low welfare is driven by limited access to decent job packages (rather than a high preference for leisure), then policy levers that expand job availability or reduce rationing constraints are welfare-relevant in a different way than pure marginal-tax changes. fileciteturn6file16 fileciteturn6file12 In your baseline, the argument is not abstract: the opportunity-sensitive welfare Gini exceeds the disposable-income Gini (0.186 vs 0.164), and equalizing opportunities alone reduces welfare inequality materially (to 0.151), implying a quantitatively important opportunity component (0.039 Gini points; 43.8% of the explained reduction). fileciteturn3file7

Decomposition is more informative than ranking because it answers a *mechanism* question—“what component of welfare inequality is opportunity-driven, and how sensitive is that answer to responsibility assumptions?”—rather than only “who is higher/lower under metric \(W\).” fileciteturn6file16 fileciteturn3file12 Formally, your decomposition is a scientific object that survives in hostile seminar questioning: it is built from explicit counterfactual equalizations (opportunity equalized, preference neutralized, both) and then attributed with an order-invariant Shapley–Shorrocks rule rather than an arbitrary sequencing, which is exactly what makes “opportunity share of welfare inequality” a defensible quantitative claim rather than a rhetorical label. fileciteturn6file16 fileciteturn5file2

## Identification in two sentences

The identification challenge is that observed job-package choices conflate **(i) how individuals rank packages** (preferences) with **(ii) which packages are available** (latent opportunities), so a model without an explicit opportunity block will tend to re-label opportunity gradients as taste heterogeneity. fileciteturn3file6 fileciteturn4file8  
The paper addresses this by separately parameterizing the opportunity mechanism (job-offer intensity/composition varying by region × education) and the preference mechanism (age shifters and latent taste types), so the estimated model can map from “ranking vs access” into an opportunity-conditioned money-metric welfare level and a responsibility-sensitive Shapley decomposition of welfare inequality. fileciteturn6file12 fileciteturn3file12
