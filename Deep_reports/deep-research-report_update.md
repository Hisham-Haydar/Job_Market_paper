## Contribution logic and positioning

Your corpus already contains the *closest neighbors* you will be compared to in seminars. The key to a strong JMP is to be explicit about what you borrow from them and what you add that they do not deliver.

**How this differs from the closest structural labor supply papers in your corpus**  
The classic constrained-choice structural papers in your corpus already estimate labor supply as discrete job-package choice and simulate tax reforms with welfare objects like EV/CV. They are often read primarily as “structural policy evaluation” papers: they demonstrate that constrained job packages matter for behavioral incidence and welfare effects of reforms. fileciteturn1file2  
Your contribution is to make **inequality decomposition** the central object rather than a byproduct: you will take the opportunity mechanism seriously enough that it becomes an attributable driver of welfare inequality, not merely a modeling convenience that helps fit hours distributions. This reframes the opportunity model from “auxiliary structure” into a measurable inequality mechanism.

**How this differs from the closest welfare-comparison / heterogeneous-preference papers in your corpus**  
You already have papers that compute welfare measures under heterogeneous preferences and even compare welfare implications across countries or groups. The risk with that literature, and the reason your supervisor warns against simple rankings, is that “the welfare number moves” is not itself a mechanism. Welfare rankings also tend to be sensitive to metric choices, and they can look incremental unless you introduce a new primitive. fileciteturn1file2  
Your opportunities framework *is* that new primitive: it allows you to say that (a) some inequality arises because individuals face different feasible job sets and not because they value leisure differently, and (b) the welfare metric must incorporate the opportunity constraint consistently or it will misclassify constrained individuals as “choosing low-welfare bundles.”

**Why the opportunities angle is scientifically meaningful (not rhetorical)**  
When feasible job sets differ across individuals, observed hours/wages bundles do not map cleanly into preferences: the same observed bundle may be a chosen optimum for one individual and a constrained second-best for another. If you ignore this, structural preference heterogeneity becomes a residual category that absorbs opportunity inequality. This contaminates both behavioral counterfactuals and welfare conclusions. Your corpus already treats this issue explicitly in multiple ways (latent jobs identification, restricted choices, rationing models, and warnings on feasible-set representations). fileciteturn1file2

**Why decomposition is a stronger JMP contribution than ranking**  
A ranking answers “who looks better under metric \(W\).” A decomposition answers “what drives inequality in \(W\), and what would change it.” Rankings can be interesting, but they are often read as “measurement exercises” unless they uncover a new structural mechanism. Decomposition, especially if it is built on counterfactual equalizations of the opportunity mechanism and is made order-independent, is closer to a scientific claim: it produces testable comparative statics and policy-relevant levers (equalize opportunities; reduce constraints; change job availability) rather than a league table. This is exactly the kind of “real contribution” signal your supervisor is pushing. fileciteturn1file0

**Why this topic signals the right skills for a JMP**  
This project simultaneously demonstrates: (i) technical command of constrained discrete labor supply estimation with an explicit opportunity mechanism; (ii) ability to define and compute defensible money-metric welfare objects from structural models; (iii) competence in inequality analysis via decompositions that are interpretable under responsibility-sensitive normative criteria; and (iv) auxiliary competence in tax-benefit microsimulation as an input into disposable income and reform counterfactuals. fileciteturn1file0 fileciteturn1file2

## First empirical prototype design

The aim of the first prototype is not “full paper quality.” The aim is to produce a *seminar-credible proof of concept* that (a) opportunity parameters can be estimated in your setting, and (b) the resulting welfare inequality decomposition yields a striking, interpretable pattern.

**Unit of analysis**  
Household (or individual) \(i\) choosing one job package \(j\) from a latent feasible set \(J_i\), with job packages defined by discrete hours categories, wage offer (or earnings), and a small number of job-attribute proxies. A couples model is feasible and consistent with several templates in your corpus, but an individual model may be preferred for a first prototype if computation is binding. fileciteturn1file2

**Likely sample (prototype-level choice)**  
Single country, working-age, excluding self-employed; optionally restrict to prime-age to avoid retirement/education transitions in the first pass. The immediate goal is a dataset with enough information to proxy job packages (hours category, earnings/wage, sector/contract or similar) and with a tax-benefit calculator available for disposable income. Your corpus already contains feasible examples (Norway administrative couples; Belgium applications; EU-style survey + tax-benefit). fileciteturn1file2

**Key estimated objects**  
- **Preferences:** parameters of \(u(C_{ij}, L_{ij}, X_i; \theta_i)\) where \(C_{ij}\) is disposable income under job \(j\) and tax-benefit rules; \(L_{ij}\) is leisure implied by hours; \(X_i\) observed heterogeneity shifters; and \(\theta_i\) unobserved heterogeneity (random coefficients or discrete types).  
- **Opportunity mechanism:** a parsimonious parameterization (or semi-parametric approximation) of the distribution of feasible job packages \(J_i\) as a function of circumstances \(S_i\) (region × education × demographic type), potentially with latent intensities for job categories (full-time/part-time/nonemployment; sector if included).  
- **Wage/earnings offer structure:** if needed, a wage offer equation tied to job packages, or a joint model where wages are attributes of latent jobs rather than exogenous continuous choices. fileciteturn1file2

**Key welfare object (prototype choice)**  
Money-metric welfare level or change computed from the estimated model, such as an equivalent-variation–style measure built from the structural utility representation. In a prototype, you can define a welfare level relative to a reference job package and tax system; the key is that the welfare level is computed **conditional on the opportunity constraint** rather than assuming a universal feasible set. fileciteturn1file2

**Key decomposition object**  
Inequality index of individual (or household) welfare levels \(W_i\) (or welfare changes \(\Delta W_i\)) and a decomposition into components attributable to:  
- opportunity heterogeneity (differences in opportunity distributions across circumstances),  
- preference heterogeneity (differences in \(\theta_i\) across individuals),  
- and optionally tax-benefit schedule variation (if the prototype includes a policy counterfactual).  
Operationally, define counterfactuals that “equalize” (reassign) the opportunity mechanism across circumstance groups while holding preferences fixed, and analogously “equalize” preference heterogeneity or hold it at a reference. Then attribute inequality shares using an order-independent decomposition rule, so results are not an artifact of the order in which you strip away components. fileciteturn1file0

**What the first figure or table could look like**  
A single table (or one figure plus one table) is enough for a prototype:

- **Table:** baseline welfare inequality index; inequality under (i) opportunity equalization, (ii) preference neutralization/referencing, (iii) both; plus implied percentage reduction relative to baseline.  
- **Figure:** bar chart of decomposition shares (opportunities vs preferences vs residual/interaction) for one welfare-inequality index; optionally stratified by circumstance definition (region-only vs region×education) to show that opportunity inequality is not a trivial grouping artifact.

This will immediately demonstrate whether your core claim is plausible: “a nontrivial fraction of welfare inequality is opportunity-driven, and standard models would misattribute it to preferences.” fileciteturn1file0

## Decision memo

**Provisional title**  
Opportunity Constraints as a Driver of Welfare Inequality: A Latent-Jobs Structural Decomposition Approach

**Core question**  
How much welfare inequality in a labor-supply setting is driven by unequal job opportunities rather than by preference heterogeneity, once labor supply is modeled as discrete job-package choice with latent opportunity sets?

**Why this matters**  
Structural labor supply is routinely used to compute welfare effects of reforms, yet the opportunity environment is often treated as implicit or as a modeling convenience. If job opportunities are unequal and partly unobserved, then both preference estimates and welfare evaluations can be systematically distorted, and inequality analysis can confuse constrained choice with heterogeneous tastes. Your supervisor’s guidance suggests this mechanism is underexploited in empirically grounded welfare work and that responsibility for opportunities makes it normatively central rather than technical detail. fileciteturn1file0

**Main contribution**  
A unified pipeline that treats opportunities consistently across:  
1) estimation (recover opportunity mechanism and preferences),  
2) welfare evaluation (money-metric welfare defined under constrained feasible sets),  
3) inequality decomposition (opportunity vs preference contributions to welfare inequality).  
The signature result is a decomposition of welfare inequality into an opportunity component and a preference component, under a transparent responsibility stance.

**Empirical strategy**  
Estimate a RURO/latent-jobs discrete choice model of job-package selection using microdata and tax-benefit simulation for disposable income. Construct welfare levels (or welfare changes under a reform/counterfactual opportunity equalization) directly from the estimated model. Implement counterfactual equalizations of the opportunity mechanism across circumstance groups and use an order-independent decomposition to attribute welfare inequality. Deliver the first proof-of-concept as a small set of figures/tables and then expand to robustness and an optional reform counterfactual.

**Relation to literature**  
Builds on constrained discrete-choice labor supply and latent-jobs identification templates in your corpus, but shifts the headline object from “welfare effects of a reform” or “cross-country welfare ranking” to “structural decomposition of welfare inequality into opportunity-driven versus preference-driven components.” It also operationalizes the responsibility-for-opportunities idea that is present in your corpus’ welfare/fairness measurement strand, without turning the JMP into a pure theory paper. fileciteturn1file2 fileciteturn1file0

**Main risk**  
Separating preferences from opportunities can be fragile if the opportunity mechanism is weakly identified in the available data, and decomposition results can look mechanical if counterfactual equalizations are not clearly justified. A second risk is that the welfare metric can be attacked as normative; this is mitigated by (i) explicitly stating the responsibility stance and (ii) presenting sensitivity analysis (e.g., alternative reference preferences or alternative welfare indices) while keeping opportunities treated consistently.

**Immediate next step**  
Implement the prototype pipeline with a minimal job-package definition (hours categories × wage/earnings categories, optionally sector) and a simple opportunity mechanism varying by a small set of circumstances (region × education). Produce one baseline decomposition figure and one robustness check (alternative circumstance partition or alternative opportunity parameterization). If the opportunity share of welfare inequality is nontrivial and stable, the JMP topic is locked. fileciteturn1file0
