# Targeted Missing Literature for an Opportunity-Sensitive Decomposition of Welfare Inequality

## Corpus diagnosis

After scanning your uploaded corpus, the underrepresented margin is **not** the structural RURO / latent-jobs side. That side is already comparatively strong. The thinner layer is the one you now need for the paper’s *headline contribution*: exact decomposition methodology, empirical inequality-of-opportunity decomposition that can be adapted to a welfare object, and responsibility-sensitive measurement papers that clarify what is being compensated versus what is being respected. The most important additions therefore sit in three narrow places:  
first, the **pre-2013 decomposition foundations** behind the Shapley-Shorrocks move;  
second, the **empirical EOp decomposition papers** that split total inequality into unfair/fair or circumstance/effort components;  
third, the **well-being / welfare-loss papers** that move the object away from raw income and closer to the kind of welfare construct your JMP wants to study. citeturn8search3turn7view0turn2search7turn14view3turn13search8turn6search13

## Why these still matter for your exact project

These papers matter because your JMP is no longer asking whether opportunities matter *in general*; it is asking how to **measure and decompose welfare inequality** once opportunities are made structural and latent. For that, Shorrocks-Sastre-Chantreuil provide the decomposition grammar; Checchi-Peragine, Van de gaer-Ramos, and Peichl-Ungerer provide the closest empirical templates for turning total inequality into an ethically structured object; and Calo-Blanco-García-Pérez, Mahler-Ramos, Hufe-Kanbur-Peichl, and Bosmans-Dormans-Öztürk bring the literature much closer to your precise ambition of moving from income inequality to welfare loss, well-being, unfair inequality, and compensation-versus-reward splits. citeturn8search3turn7view0turn7view1turn2search7turn14view1turn14view0turn14view3turn13search8turn6search13turn5view3

More concretely, these additions help on four fronts. They give you a cleaner justification for using a **Shapley-style order-independent decomposition** rather than an ad hoc swap exercise. They give you a stronger language for converting your opportunity block into a **responsibility-sensitive unfair-inequality object** rather than just another reduced-form “channel.” They force you to confront whether your welfare comparison is **ex ante or ex post**, whether it is **household- or individual-based**, and whether the relevant counterfactual neutralizes **circumstances, opportunities, or both**. And they protect the paper from the criticism that it is “just RURO plus one extra table,” by tying the decomposition to an existing fairness and EOp measurement literature that your current corpus only partially covers. citeturn6search2turn14view0turn14view1turn10search1turn13search8turn6search13turn5view3
