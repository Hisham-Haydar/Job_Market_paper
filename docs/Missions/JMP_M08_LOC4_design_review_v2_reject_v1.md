# Independent Economics/Numerical Review — JMP-M08 LOC4 Design v2

I reviewed `JMP_M08_LOC4_robustness_design_v2.md` against the binding Path-B ruling and the deputy Part-B instrument reproduced in the request. The design is substantially stronger than v1 and gets the central Monte-Carlo difference logic right. However, there is one substantive operational error in the implementation of the Path-B sign/order materiality criterion, plus several numerical specifications that should be tightened before the design can be frozen.  

**OVERALL VERDICT: REJECT — NARROW CORRECTIVE RETURN.**

Critically, this is **not a rejection of the R-a baseline-scale convention**. I affirmatively accept R-a. The rejection is caused primarily by the implementation of M-5, not by the baseline-scale rule. Therefore, on the terms of the deputy instrument, this review does **not** trigger the special deputy-return condition “review rejects the baseline-scale convention.”

## A1 — Difference instrument

**VERDICT: ACCEPT-WITH-DISCLOSURES.**

The central construction is correct and statistically preferable to an arm-by-arm uncertainty calculation.

The design faithfully implements the deputy's R-a instruction. The scale used to assess LOC4 precision is fixed from the baseline arm rather than being allowed to respond to the LOC4 result itself. This is important. For example,

$$
S_{k0}
=
\max\left\{
|\bar W_{k,0}|,\,
|\operatorname{med}(W_{k,0})|,\,
IQR(W_{k,0}),\,
1
\right\}
$$

is determined entirely from the baseline. Likewise, the substantive mean criterion uses

$$
\frac{|\widehat\Delta_{\bar W}|}
{\max\{|\bar W_0|,1\}}
$$

rather than a denominator partly determined by LOC4. This eliminates an undesirable endogenous-tolerance mechanism in which a specification that changes the welfare level substantially could simultaneously make its own numerical tolerance more permissive. The design carries this distinction correctly. 

The blockwise CRN construction is also the right one:

$$
\Delta_{T,b}
=
T^{L4}_{b}-T^{0}_{b},
$$

followed by estimation of the Monte-Carlo error of the **difference itself**. Under paired Monte-Carlo evaluation,

$$
\operatorname{Var}
(T^{L4}_{b}-T^{0}_{b})
=
\operatorname{Var}(T^{L4}_{b})
+
\operatorname{Var}(T^{0}_{b})
-
2\operatorname{Cov}(T^{L4}_{b},T^{0}_{b}).
$$

With effective common random numbers the covariance term should be strongly positive because much of the simulation noise is shared. This is exactly the variance reduction CRN is intended to generate. Adding standalone-arm uncertainty bands throws that covariance information away. It can therefore make a precisely identified specification difference look numerically unresolved even when each arm separately is relatively noisy.

This also explains why the arm-precision rider is statistically coherent. There is nothing contradictory in having

$$
T_0 \quad\text{imprecisely estimated},\qquad
T_1 \quad\text{imprecisely estimated},
$$

while

$$
T_1-T_0
$$

is estimated precisely. This is the ordinary logic of paired designs: the common component of the noise cancels.

The two-layer architecture is also correct:

1. first ask whether \(\Delta\) is sufficiently precisely measured;
2. only then ask whether the identified difference clears the substantive materiality threshold.

The classification

$$
|\widehat\Delta|-E_\Delta\geq m
$$

for MATERIAL,

$$
|\widehat\Delta|+E_\Delta<m
$$

for IMMATERIAL, and the intervening region for INDETERMINATE_MC is a coherent interval-based decision rule. Applying it to the baseline-relative mean/median ratio is also correct because the denominator is fixed.

There are two qualifications.

First, the statement in §3.2 that combining arm bands “systematically overstates uncertainty” is too categorical as a general statistical proposition. The exact reason direct differencing dominates here is the positive covariance generated by the CRN construction. The memo should say something like “ignores the positive CRN covariance and therefore generally overstates uncertainty under the intended paired design.” The current wording is harmless operationally but stronger than the mathematics warrants.

Second, the memo still does not actually define \(E_\Delta\) completely. It says:

> “the block error estimate computed DIRECTLY on \(\{\Delta_{T,b}\}\)”

while OL-8 leaves the accepted replicate definition to Stage A. That identifies the object but not the estimator. Before freeze, the existing block-error formula must be transcribed literally: which \(T12_{(-b)}\) objects enter, what centering is used, what multiplier/scaling turns their dispersion into \(E_\Delta\), and whether it is the exact same error functional used for the accepted arm-level \(E_T\). Otherwise two implementations can obey “use block differences” and nevertheless produce different bands.

That is a specification-completeness issue, not a rejection of the CRN design.

## A2 — Classification and arm-precision rider

**VERDICT: REJECT.**

The numerical MATERIAL/IMMATERIAL/INDETERMINATE boundaries are correct. The arm-precision rider is also correct. The defect is specifically the operational definition of **M-5, sign/order change**.

Path-B says that LOC4 is materially different if:

> “the sign or ordering of a headline decomposition component changes.” 

Part B then says:

> “Sign and ordering changes must agree across all accepted block replicates.”

That means the object to be checked is an **arm-to-arm change in the qualitative configuration of the decomposition**.

For a component \(c\), a sign change is something like

$$
\operatorname{sgn}(\phi^{L4}_{c,b})
\neq
\operatorname{sgn}(\phi^{0}_{c,b}).
$$

For two components \(c,d\), an ordering change is something like

$$
\operatorname{sgn}
(\phi^{L4}_{c,b}-\phi^{L4}_{d,b})
\neq
\operatorname{sgn}
(\phi^{0}_{c,b}-\phi^{0}_{d,b}).
$$

The same change/no-change verdict must then be stable over all accepted paired block replicates.

The design instead states in §3.5:

$$
\operatorname{sign}(\Delta_{T,b})
$$

must be stable across replicates, and it compares the ordering of the **differences** themselves. 

Those are different economic propositions.

For example, suppose

$$
\phi_A^0=0.10,\qquad
\phi_A^{L4}=0.12.
$$

Then

$$
\Delta\phi_A=+0.02,
$$

but there has plainly been **no sign change**: the component is positive in both specifications.

Likewise suppose

$$
\phi_A^0=0.10,\quad\phi_B^0=0.20,
$$

and

$$
\phi_A^{L4}=0.12,\quad\phi_B^{L4}=0.21.
$$

The differences are \(0.02\) and \(0.01\), so their ordering is

$$
\Delta\phi_A>\Delta\phi_B.
$$

But the economically relevant component ordering remains

$$
\phi_A<\phi_B
$$

in both arms. There has been no Path-B ordering reversal.

Conversely, a genuine ordering reversal can occur even if the ordering of \(\Delta\phi_A\) and \(\Delta\phi_B\) does not convey it transparently.

This is therefore not merely wording. It changes the M-5 event and can generate both false materiality findings and missed materiality findings.

The correct blockwise implementation should be:

$$
S_{c,b}
=
1\{
\operatorname{sgn}(\phi^{L4}_{c,b})
\neq
\operatorname{sgn}(\phi^{0}_{c,b})
\},
$$

and, for ordering,

$$
O_{cd,b}
=
1\{
\operatorname{sgn}(\phi^{L4}_{c,b}-\phi^{L4}_{d,b})
\neq
\operatorname{sgn}(\phi^{0}_{c,b}-\phi^{0}_{d,b})
\}.
$$

Then:

* MATERIAL on M-5 if the full T16 comparison exhibits the change and the same change is present in every accepted replicate;
* IMMATERIAL on M-5 if the full T16 comparison exhibits no change and every accepted replicate likewise exhibits no change;
* INDETERMINATE_MC if the block replicates disagree about the change/no-change status.

A deterministic convention is also needed for an exact zero or exact tie. No substantive numerical cutoff should be invented, but the computational definition of `sign(0)` and equality must be explicit.

The M-6 treatment is otherwise good. Pre-registering the qualitative paper conclusion before looking at LOC4 is exactly what is needed to prevent post hoc reinterpretation.

The arm rider itself passes. In particular, allowing a verdict on `W1_s_opp` despite the standalone baseline level failing its own precision gate is statistically legitimate if the paired \(\Delta s_{\rm opp}\) passes its direct precision gate and all validity/comparability conditions pass. The memo handles this distinction well. 

## A3 — Path-B reconciliation

**VERDICT: REJECT, solely because M-5 is not operationally carried correctly; otherwise the reconciliation is substantively sound.**

The §4 Path-B contract is, in almost every other respect, correctly reconciled.

The existing four-category LOC4 grouping is retained. No grouping search is introduced. Sex-specific occupation densities are excluded. Industry and external regional covariates remain out. This is faithful to the ruling. 

The model-form perturbation is also appropriately narrow:

$$
\mu_i(X,k)
=
\mu_i(X)+\delta_k,\qquad
\delta_1=0,
$$

with a common \(\sigma\). Thus LOC4 modifies occupation-specific **wage location**, not occupation-specific dispersion. The design explicitly documents this, as Path-B requires. 

There is an unfortunate wording tension in Path-B itself: its economic rationale twice describes the open issue as “occupation-specific wage dispersion,” whereas its operative §4 contract asks the mission to document “whether mean and/or dispersion changes are introduced.”  Given the design's cited M3 definition—occupation-specific Mincer locations with common \(\sigma\)—the mean-only reading is internally coherent. But every paper-facing description should call this a **four-density occupation-specific wage-location robustness**, not a test of occupation-specific \(\sigma_k\). The memo largely already observes that distinction.

The full re-estimation reconciliation is also economically correct. Path-B §3's statement that no parameter is re-estimated “inside the welfare mission” describes the closed baseline welfare prototype. The LOC4 robustness mission is a separate model-estimation exercise. A structurally meaningful LOC4 comparison requires maximizing the likelihood of the LOC4 specification:

$$
\widehat\theta_{L4}
=
\arg\max_\theta \ell_{L4}(\theta),
$$

rather than inserting three \(\delta_k\)'s while holding all other baseline coefficients fixed. The latter would be a hybrid parameter vector and would not represent the fitted LOC4 model. The memo's “form preserved, coefficients re-estimated” reconciliation is the appropriate econometric interpretation.

The double-counting argument is also basically sound. Writing the change in the occupation-\(k\) lognormal wage density as

$$
\Delta\log f_W
=
\frac{\delta_k}{\sigma^2}(\log w-\mu_i)
-
\frac{\delta_k^2}{2\sigma^2},
$$

shows the decomposition clearly.

The second term is constant in \(w\) within occupation. An occupation intercept in \(O^{Occ}\) can absorb that level shift. By contrast,

$$
\frac{\delta_k}{\sigma^2}\log w
$$

generates a within-occupation wage gradient that an occupation intercept cannot reproduce. Economically, the distinction is:

$$
\beta^{Occ}_k:
\text{relative mass/availability of occupation }k,
$$

versus

$$
\delta_k:
\text{location of wages conditional on occupation }k.
$$

So the substantive identification argument is legitimate.

I would nevertheless weaken the statement that the constant term is “assigned unambiguously” to the access channel. More precisely, once the \(w\)-dependent variation identifies \(\delta_k\), its implied constant term is mechanically determined and the free occupation intercept absorbs the remaining level. That is more accurate than suggesting that the constant term independently identifies an economic access effect.

The Path-B quantitative thresholds are also correctly transcribed:

$$
|\Delta s_{\rm opp}|\ge .02,
$$

$$
|\Delta G|\ge .005,
$$

$$
|\Delta\bar W|/|\bar W_0|\ge .01,
\qquad
|\Delta\operatorname{med}W|/
|\operatorname{med}W_0|\ge .01.
$$

No component-level threshold is invented. That is correct.

But §5.4 of Path-B is part of the substantive battery. Because M-5 is currently operationalized incorrectly, I cannot certify the statement that the Path-B materiality rule has been carried “clause-by-clause.” Fixing §3.5 and the corresponding branch language should cure this without reopening the economic design.

## A4 — Estimation-side soundness

**VERDICT: ACCEPT-WITH-DISCLOSURES, but several items must be bound before freeze/execution.**

The basic identification argument for \(\delta_{occ}\) is valid. Conditional on occupation, variation in \(\log w\) distinguishes a wage-location parameter from an occupation intercept. The important identifying moment is not simply the presence of observations from multiple occupations; it is wage support **within each non-reference occupation**, together with sufficient variation after conditioning on the rest of the structural index.

Thus the relevant rank condition is conceptually of the form

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\log w-\mu_i\mid k)>0,
$$

for \(k=2,3,4\), plus absence of exact collinearity with the other estimated terms.

The full simultaneous re-estimation is appropriate. The CR1 finite-sample factor is also correctly updated. With household clustering and \(G=N=1555\),

$$
c
=
\frac{G}{G-1}
\frac{N-1}{N-K_{\rm free}}
=
\frac{1555}{1555-K_{\rm free}}.
$$

It is correct not to recycle the baseline numerical correction after the free dimension changes.

The inference treatment of active-bound parameters is internally consistent with the project's established convention: exclude them from the regular covariance block and report SE/z/p as literal NA. Econometrically, true boundary inference is non-standard rather than ordinary Gaussian inference, so the memo is right not to fabricate conventional standard errors for active coordinates.

The convergence architecture is sensible: full optimization, no tolerance relaxation because of the extra coordinates, single-optimum requirement, and return rather than cherry-picking when the surface is genuinely multi-optimum. However, OL-3 is still important: because the actual four legs are absent from this review packet, I can certify the architecture but **not the literal convergence test** until those legs and the deputy class-(c) rule are transcribed.

G-L4-3 through G-L4-7 are strong gates. In particular, proposal invariance, geometry/reference invariance, support invariance, EUROMOD comparability, and complete normalization revalidation are precisely the sorts of gates needed for a credible paired welfare comparison.

G-L4-8, however, is too weak if it consists only of a fitted correlation table. A small or large estimated correlation is not by itself an identification test. The LOC4 estimation return should also establish local curvature/rank for the extended \((\beta^{Occ},\delta)\) block. Ideally this is already part of the inherited four-leg/Hessian battery; if so, v2 should say explicitly that G-L4-8 is read jointly with that Hessian/rank/condition evidence. A nearly singular \((\beta^{Occ},\delta)\) block cannot simply be called an interesting finding while still supporting paper-facing LOC4 welfare magnitudes.

There is also one numerical correction needed in the C-8 discussion.

The memo correctly notes that the deterministic chosen-node numerator cancels:

$$
\frac{y_0+\sum_{t=1}^{n}y^{L4}_t}{n+1}
-
\frac{y_0+\sum_{t=1}^{n}y^{0}_t}{n+1}
=
\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{n}(y^{L4}_t-y^0_t)}{n+1}.
$$

But the upstream A5-i normalization defect itself does **not** fully cancel. Relative to the nominal \(1/n\) estimator, the difference remains multiplied by

$$
\frac{n}{n+1}.
$$

At T16 this is

$$
\frac{1600}{1601}\approx0.999375,
$$

and analogous factors differ slightly at T12 and T8. Therefore the proper statement is:

* the additive deterministic chosen-node term cancels exactly under bitwise identity;
* the \(n/(n+1)\) normalization inheritance remains in the difference;
* LOC4 is nevertheless required to inherit that estimator because it is the accepted estimator of record;
* if that upstream estimator is repaired, LOC4 must be rerun.

Numerically this residual factor is very small relative to the LOC4 substantive and MC thresholds, so I do not regard it as grounds for redesign. But “the defect cancels” would be false; only one part of the defect cancels.

The unresolved \(\delta_{occ}\) bound deserves tightening as well. The present rule contains the phrase “with slack,” which leaves discretion. A pre-registered structural robustness should freeze either an exact \(B\) or an exact deterministic formula for \(B\), including the slack amount, **before optimization**. This matters because bounds determine the admissible likelihood region and potentially the interior covariance set.

Finally, the partial-CRN branch is defensible as a diagnostic regime, but should be described carefully. If the defensive box moves, the experiment is no longer fully paired at the realized-node level. The resulting \(E_\Delta\) can still be estimated directly from matched replicate labels with partial common randomness, but it should not be advertised as the same full-CRN precision experiment. The memo already mostly does this correctly.

# Final disposition

The design gets the most important new Part-B economics/numerics right:

* **R-a fixed baseline scaling: ACCEPTED.**
* **Direct blockwise differencing: ACCEPTED.**
* **Difference precision rather than summed arm bands: ACCEPTED.**
* **Two-layer precision/materiality logic: ACCEPTED.**
* **Arm-precision rider: ACCEPTED.**
* **Mean-only LOC4 with common \(\sigma\): ACCEPTED as the specified first LOC4 axis.**
* **Full re-estimation and the basic \(\delta_{occ}\)/\(\beta_{occ}\) identification argument: ACCEPTED.**
* **Path-B numerical thresholds: ACCEPTED.**

The blocking defect is specific and repairable: **M-5 currently tests the sign/order of LOC4-minus-baseline differences instead of testing whether the signs/orderings of the decomposition components change between the two specifications.** That directly changes one of the Path-B materiality events and therefore propagates into Branch A/B/C.

Before a new review, I would require exactly these corrections:

1. Replace §3.5 with an arm-to-arm sign/order change test, evaluated within each paired replicate.
2. Propagate that corrected definition through M-5, §§4.1–4.5, and any C-9 language that currently relies on the erroneous \(\Delta\)-ordering formulation.
3. Transcribe the exact existing block-error estimator defining \(E_\Delta\), including the accepted replicate family and scaling, rather than leaving “block error estimate” generic.
4. Correct C-8 to state that the chosen-node numerator cancels but the \(n/(n+1)\) normalization factor does not.
5. Tie G-L4-8 explicitly to rank/curvature/conditioning evidence for the extended \((\beta^{Occ},\delta)\) block, not correlation alone.
6. Freeze an exact \(B\), or an exact deterministic bound rule including the numerical slack, before estimation.

With those corrections, I see no economic or numerical reason to reject the R-a difference design itself. As submitted, however, the M-5 defect means the memo cannot yet serve as the frozen executable materiality contract.

**OVERALL: REJECT — baseline-scale convention explicitly ACCEPTED; rejection is for the M-5 implementation and freeze-completeness issues above.**
