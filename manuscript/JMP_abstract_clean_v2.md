## Abstract

This paper asks whether standard structural labour-supply models overstate preference heterogeneity because unequal job opportunities are left implicit, and what this implies for the decomposition of well-being inequality. The empirical application is a random-utility random-opportunity model of latent job packages estimated on France 2016 single-adult households from SRCV / EUROMOD-input microdata, in the P2a region-live specification with 1,555 households. Households do not choose hours from a common menu: they choose among job packages that differ in the hours they carry, in the occupation they belong to, and in the regional labour market in which they are located, and whose availability differs across observable circumstances. The paper's contribution is a single empirical framework that treats opportunities consistently in estimation, in welfare evaluation, and in decomposition. It estimates preferences jointly with an access mechanism for discrete job opportunities and a wage density through which ability-related characteristics operate; it uses tax-benefit microsimulation only to map job packages into disposable income; it constructs money-metric household welfare conditional on the constrained feasible set; and it decomposes welfare inequality by counterfactual equalization and a Shapley-Shorrocks rule into an opportunity-attributable component and a preference-related component, with the opportunity component split further into access and ability. In the estimated model the regional access block is jointly relevant, and measured access heterogeneity is concentrated in one GSUR dimension rather than diffuse across broad NUTS-1 geography or the two urbanisation indicators. [[WELFARE AND DECOMPOSITION MAGNITUDES — PLACEHOLDER, pending M08 acceptance and the pre-registered LOC4 wage-density robustness.]] The paper is therefore neither a welfare ranking nor a reform evaluation; its central object is the share of welfare inequality attributable to unequal opportunities relative to preference heterogeneity, read under a responsibility-sensitive interpretation in which unequal opportunities are compensation-relevant circumstances — an interpretive stance of the paper rather than a property established by the estimates. Identification is demanding in this class of models, so the contribution is disciplined measurement in one concrete implementation rather than a claim of already-settled magnitudes. The empirical analysis first establishes the complete estimation–welfare–decomposition pipeline for France 2016 single-adult households; extension to couples and pooled years is subsequent work.
