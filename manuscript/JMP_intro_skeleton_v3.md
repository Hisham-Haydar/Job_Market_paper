## 1. Opening motivation paragraph

**Purpose:** Open the paper with the broad empirical and welfare problem, and make clear from the start that the paper is about the sources of welfare inequality rather than about a new policy simulation or a country ranking.

Structural labour-supply models are widely used to estimate preferences and evaluate policy, but they often treat labour supply as a choice from a common hours menu under a household-specific budget set. In many labour markets, however, households do not choose hours directly; they choose among jobs, and the set of feasible job opportunities differs across households. Once this is recognised, the interpretation of observed labour-market outcomes becomes less straightforward, because the same hours–income bundle may reflect unconstrained choice for one household and a constrained second-best for another. This paper starts from the concern that, if opportunity heterogeneity is left implicit, structural models will tend to absorb it into preference heterogeneity. That concern matters both for positive interpretation and for welfare analysis, and it motivates an empirical design in which opportunities are treated as a first-class object rather than as background structure. ## 2. Why opportunities matter paragraph

**Purpose:** Explain why the distinction between opportunities and preferences is economically substantive, not just a modelling refinement, and connect that point to the paper's responsibility-sensitive interpretation.

This paragraph should explain why unequal opportunities matter for welfare analysis, not only for labour-supply estimation. If two households reach similar observed outcomes but face different feasible job sets, then treating both outcomes as pure preference revelation is misleading. In that case, some of what looks like taste heterogeneity may instead reflect unequal access to work opportunities, hours packages, or occupations, or unequal earning capacity within the packages that are reachable. This is also where the paper introduces its limited normative stance: unequal opportunities are interpreted as compensation-relevant circumstances, while preference heterogeneity is kept conceptually separate rather than being allowed to absorb constraints. The paragraph should be explicit that this stance is an interpretation the paper adopts and defends, not a label that the estimated components carry by themselves. It should stay empirical in tone and should not drift into the separate theory paper on axiomatic well-being measurement. [cite latent jobs paper] [cite responsibility paper] [cite restricted choices paper]

## 3. Core question paragraph

**Purpose:** State the main question sharply, in three-way access/ability/preference language, and make decomposition rather than ranking the central object.

The core question paragraph should state the paper's main question in one clean sentence: how much of observed inequality in money-metric well-being is attributable to unequal job opportunities rather than to heterogeneous preferences, once labour supply is modelled as discrete choice among latent job packages and welfare is evaluated under constrained feasible sets. A second sentence should unpack the opportunity environment into its two channels — access, which governs which job packages are available to whom, and ability, which governs the wage an individual commands within the packages available — while preferences govern how a household ranks consumption–leisure bundles among the packages it can reach. The paragraph should then be explicit about the ordering of the empirical object: the headline cut separates the opportunity environment from preferences, and the access-versus-ability split is a nested supporting decomposition inside the opportunity component rather than a competing headline. A further sentence should add the sharper empirical interpretation: standard structural labour-supply models may overstate preference heterogeneity because they leave unequal job opportunities implicit. The paragraph should close by saying that the paper is organised neither around country ranking nor around a list of policy simulations, but around the mechanism generating welfare inequality, and that the empirical object is the decomposition of household welfare inequality. [cite latent jobs paper] [cite decomposition paper]

## 4. Literature gap paragraph

**Purpose:** Sharpen the literature gap by identifying the three relevant neighbouring literatures and the precise missing combination that the paper supplies.

This paragraph should be very explicit that the gap is not "nobody has studied opportunities" and not "nobody has studied welfare." The first neighbouring literature is constrained structural labour supply, including RURO and latent-jobs approaches, which already show that jobs should be modelled as packages and that opportunity densities matter for behaviour, but usually treat welfare evaluation as an endpoint rather than making decomposition central. The second neighbouring literature is empirical welfare analysis under heterogeneous preferences, which shows that money-metric welfare comparisons are sensitive to the treatment of preferences, but typically does not model latent job opportunities explicitly. The third neighbouring literature is inequality-of-opportunity and responsibility-sensitive measurement, which provides the language of circumstances and compensation, but usually does not rest on a structural labour-supply model with estimated opportunities and tax-benefit-consistent budget mappings. The sharp gap is therefore the absence of a single empirical pipeline that estimates opportunities, carries them into welfare measurement, and then decomposes welfare inequality in an order-independent way. [cite latent jobs paper] [cite welfare paper] [cite inequality of opportunity paper] [cite decomposition paper]

## 5. Contribution paragraph

**Purpose:** State the paper's contribution as a sequence of linked steps and make clear that the novelty is decomposition, not merely another structural model or another welfare metric.

The contribution paragraph should be written as a three-step logic. First, the paper estimates a structural labour-supply model with constrained opportunities and latent job packages, in which access to packages and earning capacity within them are modelled explicitly rather than treated as residual tastes. Second, it constructs a money-metric household welfare object defined relative to constrained feasible sets rather than to a universal choice set. Third, it decomposes the household welfare Gini into an opportunity component and a preference component using a Shapley-Shorrocks rule, and then splits the opportunity component into its access and ability parts as a nested supporting result. A useful closing sentence is that the novelty is not "one more structural labour-supply model" and not "one more welfare-ranking exercise," but the integration of opportunities, welfare and decomposition into one empirical design. That sentence should also keep the paper distinct from the separate pure theory paper by emphasising that the present project is an empirical structural JMP. [cite latent jobs paper] [cite welfare paper] [cite decomposition paper]

## 6. France empirical design paragraph

**Purpose:** Make the France grounding unmistakable, name the accepted application precisely, and signal that the empirical prototype is real, narrow, and feasible without overstating what has been delivered.

This paragraph should say clearly that the empirical application is a **France 2016** structural labour-supply model based on **SRCV / EUROMOD-input microdata**, estimated on **single-adult households** in the **P2a region-live specification**, contributing **1,555 household observations**. The unit of welfare analysis is the household, which in this application is single-adult, and the first decomposition is defined over household welfare levels rather than individual wage-hours outcomes. At the behavioural level, each individual faces a non-work option together with sampled latent job packages that differ in the hours they carry, in the occupation they belong to, and in the regional labour market in which they are located, with wages governed by an estimated log-wage density shifted by education and potential experience; household disposable income for each sampled alternative is computed through EUROMOD. The welfare object is money-metric household welfare relative to a non-work reference state, and the baseline inequality object is the Gini of household welfare. The paragraph should then state, briefly and in bounded wording, what the estimated model already establishes: the regional access block is jointly relevant and, at the resolution and specification studied, measured access heterogeneity is concentrated in one GSUR dimension rather than diffuse across broad NUTS-1 geography or the two urbanisation indicators — with the accompanying caution that a failure to reject the remaining restrictions is not evidence that those coefficients are zero, that no substantive label is attached to the GSUR coordinate beyond its design-column identity, and that none of this is a causal claim about location and employment. It should acknowledge, without dramatising it, that inference is conditional on the observed active set and that the welfare stage carries a pre-registered local sensitivity for two near-boundary coordinates, so the first version of the paper is a disciplined and deliberately narrow prototype rather than a claim of fully settled magnitudes. One closing sentence handles scope, and it is the authorized staged-extension sentence used verbatim and without addition: "The empirical analysis first establishes the complete estimation–welfare–decomposition pipeline for France 2016 single-adult households; extension to couples and pooled years is subsequent work." ## 7. Roadmap paragraph

**Purpose:** Tell the reader exactly how the rest of the paper is organised and reinforce the centrality of decomposition.

The roadmap paragraph can start by saying that the next section presents the structural framework and clarifies how preferences, access and earning capacity enter the latent-jobs choice problem. The following section describes the France 2016 single-adult sample, the sampled job representation, and the estimation strategy based on RURO with tax-benefit-consistent disposable income. The next section reports estimation results and inference, including the regional-access hypothesis battery, the conditional covariance and its `NA` convention for bounded and pinned coordinates, and the pre-registered diagnostics that route into the welfare stage. A subsequent section defines the money-metric household welfare object and explains why welfare is evaluated relative to constrained feasible sets rather than to a universal choice set. The next section presents the decomposition strategy, focusing on the household welfare Gini, the Shapley-Shorrocks attribution to opportunity and preference components, and the nested access/ability split. A final sentence can say that the empirical results section culminates in one main table and one main figure designed to show whether the opportunity component of welfare inequality is empirically meaningful under the baseline France prototype, with the pre-registered wage-density robustness completed before any preferred magnitude is stated as the paper's headline number, and further robustness exercises postponed until after that baseline result is established.
