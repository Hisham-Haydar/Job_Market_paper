# Opportunity Constraints as a Driver of Welfare Inequality: A Latent-Jobs Structural Decomposition Approach

## 1. Motivation

Structural labour-supply models are commonly used to estimate preferences and evaluate policy, but they usually treat labour supply as a choice from a common hours menu under a household-specific budget set. In many labour markets, however, individuals do not choose hours directly. They choose among jobs, and the set of feasible job opportunities differs across workers and households. Once this is recognised, observed labour-market outcomes become normatively ambiguous: the same hours–income bundle may reflect unconstrained choice for one household and a constrained second-best for another. The central concern of this project is that, if opportunity heterogeneity is left implicit, structural models will tend to absorb it into preference heterogeneity. That distorts both the positive interpretation of labour-supply behaviour and the welfare analysis built on top of it. The paper is therefore motivated by the need to treat opportunities as a first-class empirical object and to quantify their contribution to welfare inequality.

## 2. Main research question

The paper asks how much observed inequality in money-metric well-being is attributable to unequal job opportunities rather than to heterogeneous preferences, once labour supply is modelled as discrete choice among latent job packages and welfare is evaluated conditional on constrained feasible sets. The question is posed in three-way language. The opportunity environment operates through **access**, which governs which job packages are available to whom, and through **ability**, which governs the wage an individual commands within the packages that are available; **preferences** govern how a household ranks consumption–leisure bundles among the packages it can reach. The first and headline empirical cut separates the opportunity environment from preferences; the access-versus-ability split is a nested supporting decomposition inside the opportunity component. Put differently, the empirical question is whether standard structural labour-supply models overstate preference heterogeneity because unequal access to jobs is left implicit, and whether this matters quantitatively for the decomposition of welfare inequality. The paper is not organised around country ranking or reform incidence. Its central object is the mechanism generating welfare inequality.

## 3. Literature gap

The literature gap should be understood narrowly and precisely. The first relevant literature is constrained structural labour supply, including RURO and related latent-jobs approaches. That literature already shows that jobs should be modelled as packages and that opportunity densities matter for behaviour, but it typically treats welfare evaluation as an endpoint and the opportunity mechanism as part of the econometric architecture rather than as the object of decomposition. The second literature is empirical welfare analysis under heterogeneous preferences. That literature establishes that money-metric welfare comparisons are sensitive to the treatment of preference heterogeneity, but it generally does not model job opportunities explicitly, so some of what is labelled taste heterogeneity may still reflect unmodelled opportunity inequality. The third literature is inequality of opportunity and responsibility-sensitive measurement. That literature supplies the normative language of circumstances and compensation, but it usually does not rest on a structural labour-supply model with estimated latent opportunities and tax-benefit-consistent budget mappings. The gap, therefore, is not the absence of these ingredients separately; it is the absence of a single empirical pipeline that estimates opportunities, carries them into welfare measurement, and then decomposes welfare inequality in an order-independent way. That is the specific contribution space of the paper.

## 4. Core mechanism: opportunities versus preferences

The empirical mechanism separates preferences from opportunities. Preferences govern how a household ranks consumption–leisure bundles conditional on available jobs. Opportunities govern which job packages are feasible, and what a given individual can earn within them, and how both vary across observable circumstances. In reduced form, the choice problem can be written so that utility from a sampled alternative depends on disposable income and leisure, while an explicit opportunity term shifts the attractiveness of that alternative through its availability. If the opportunity term is omitted, the model is forced to reinterpret constrained outcomes as tastes. That is the central identification and interpretation problem. The project therefore treats opportunities consistently across the behavioural and welfare layers. Its normative stance is limited but explicit: unequal opportunities are interpreted as compensation-relevant circumstances, while preference heterogeneity is kept conceptually separate. This stance is a reading the paper adopts and states, not a property that the estimated components carry on their own; the paper remains an empirical structural project rather than an extension of the separate axiomatic theory paper.

## 5. France empirical setting

The empirical application is a France 2016 structural labour-supply model based on SRCV / EUROMOD-input microdata. The accepted application is the **single-adult (P2a) region-live specification**, which contributes **1,555 household observations**, and the decomposition is defined over household welfare levels; in this application each household is single-adult, so each household contributes one likelihood term. This is the application for which accepted estimation and inference evidence exists, and it is therefore the writing anchor for every paper-facing empirical statement. The empirical analysis first establishes the complete estimation–welfare–decomposition pipeline for these households; extension to couples and to pooled survey years is subsequent work, conditional on successful validation of the singles pipeline.

## 6. Empirical strategy

The empirical strategy is a France-based RURO application with sampled latent alternatives. Individuals face a non-work option together with market job packages that differ in the hours they carry, in the occupation they belong to, and in the regional labour market in which they are located; each of these channels has its own block of access parameters. Wages within packages are governed by an estimated log-wage density whose location is shifted by education and potential experience, which is the channel through which ability-related characteristics operate. Disposable income for each sampled alternative is computed through EUROMOD under French tax-benefit rules, so microsimulation is a supporting input to the budget mapping rather than the behavioural contribution of the paper. Preferences are modelled with a Box-Cox specification in consumption and leisure, with sex-specific singles leisure terms. Estimation proceeds by constrained quasi-maximum likelihood with sampled alternatives and the associated correction for sampled choice sets. The objective is not to estimate an all-encompassing model in the first pass, but to obtain a disciplined preference component, a disciplined opportunity component, and a welfare mapping sufficient to support decomposition.

Estimation and inference for this application are accepted. Inference uses a sandwich covariance estimator with likelihood contributions clustered at the household level, giving 1,555 clusters, and a finite-sample correction of the HC1/CR1 family; because each household contributes a single likelihood term, the robust standard errors are misspecification-robust rather than dependence-robust. Of the 47 coordinates in the parameter vector, ten are pinned and thirty-seven are free, of which two sit at an active upper bound; the covariance object supporting symmetric inference is therefore conditional and 35-dimensional, and the paper makes no unconditional active-set claim. On substance, the joint restriction of the ten regional, urbanisation and GSUR access coefficients to zero is rejected, so regional job-access heterogeneity belongs in the model. Within that block, the restriction on the seven NUTS-1 dummies is not rejected and the restriction on the two urbanisation indicators is not rejected, while the restriction on the single GSUR coordinate is rejected on its own. The paper's statement of this result is deliberately bounded: at the resolution and specification studied, measured access heterogeneity is concentrated in one GSUR dimension rather than diffuse across broad NUTS-1 geography or the two urbanisation indicators. A failure to reject is not evidence that the coordinates in question are zero, no substantive label is attached to the GSUR coordinate beyond its design-column identity, and none of these statements is a causal claim about the effect of location on employment.

## 7. Welfare object

The welfare object is a household-level money-metric measure implied by the estimated model and evaluated relative to a non-work reference state; in this application the household is single-adult. This matters for two reasons. First, it ensures that welfare comparisons are conditional on the constrained feasible set rather than on a universal choice set. Second, it preserves a necessary distinction between estimated behavioural utility and the welfare metric used for interpersonal comparison. In the first version of the paper the money-metric measure is the baseline object, while the alternative reference-preference measure remains a robustness exercise. The welfare stage also inherits one pre-registered diagnostic from estimation: the robust symmetric intervals of two named coordinates, the singles male leisure intercept and the potential-experience-squared coefficient in the wage-density location, approach the relevant parameter boundary. These are reported as local diagnostics and are not treated as unrestricted evidence beyond the admissible parameter region; the welfare analysis therefore includes a pre-registered admissible local sensitivity in which each flagged coordinate is perturbed by a fixed admissible amount, giving exactly four scenarios — the accepted baseline and the two single and one joint perturbations — with the sample, job draws, tax-benefit mapping, welfare normalisation, inequality index and decomposition rule held fixed. This keeps the welfare layer empirically grounded and distinct from the more abstract axiomatic issues pursued in the separate theory project.

## 8. Decomposition strategy

Decomposition is the central empirical object of the paper. The baseline inequality index is the Gini coefficient of household welfare, with the chosen headline index reported where it differs. The first-pass counterfactuals are deliberately narrow: the opportunity environment is equalized against a common reference structure, and preference heterogeneity is neutralized against a reference specification. These two counterfactuals are combined in a Shapley-Shorrocks decomposition, which attributes measured welfare inequality to an opportunity-attributable component and a preference-related component in an order-independent way, and reports the opportunity share. A nested second cut splits the opportunity component into access and ability. The exact construction of each counterfactual is fixed by the frozen welfare-input design rather than restated here. This is a stronger object than a welfare ranking: a ranking speaks to levels, while the decomposition identifies the mechanisms driving inequality in those levels. That is why decomposition, rather than ranking, is the paper's core contribution. No magnitude is reported at this stage: [[WELFARE AND DECOMPOSITION MAGNITUDES — PLACEHOLDER, pending M08 acceptance]], and no preferred magnitude is frozen before the pre-registered LOC4 wage-density robustness is completed.

## 9. Expected contribution

The paper's contribution is specific. It contributes to constrained structural labour supply by estimating job opportunities rather than treating all residual heterogeneity as tastes. It contributes to empirical welfare analysis by constructing a money-metric welfare object that is explicitly conditional on constrained opportunities. And it contributes to inequality analysis by making the decomposition of welfare inequality, rather than a welfare ranking or a reform table, the primary scientific output, including the nested separation of access from ability inside the opportunity component. The core novelty is therefore a single empirical pipeline in which opportunities are modelled in estimation, carried into welfare evaluation, and then decomposed. That is also what keeps the paper distinct from the separate theory project: the theory paper asks how well-being should be characterised axiomatically in a jobs world; this paper asks how far a concrete France implementation can go in measuring the contribution of unequal opportunities to welfare inequality.

## 10. Main risk and identification challenge

The main challenge is identification, and it should be stated concretely. The demanding part is the separation of preferences from opportunities within a structurally estimated model, and the honest position is that the product of the two is identified more cleanly than the factors. Three practical qualifications matter for how the results are read. First, all reported inference is conditional on the observed active set, since two free coordinates sit at an active bound and carry no symmetric interval; agreement between model-based and robust test verdicts is stability of those verdicts and not evidence of correct specification. Second, two interior coordinates have robust intervals that approach the relevant parameter boundary, which is disclosed as a non-gating local diagnostic and routed into the pre-registered four-scenario welfare sensitivity. Third, the certified baseline uses a single log-wage density with a common dispersion parameter, and the four-density LOC4 variant is pre-registered as the first mandatory wage-density robustness axis; it has not been estimated, no LOC4 result exists anywhere in the paper, and the preferred quantitative magnitudes are not frozen before that axis is completed. These are real constraints, but they do not make the project infeasible. They imply that the first version of the paper must remain narrow: no richer job hierarchy, no latent taste classes, and no expansive decomposition beyond the baseline. The identification difficulty is therefore a reason for discipline, not a reason to broaden the project.

## 11. First empirical prototype

The first empirical prototype is a France 2016 single-adult exercise on the accepted P2a region-live estimates. It uses the certified baseline wage specification rather than the LOC4 four-density variant; the access block of hours, occupation and regional/urbanisation/GSUR coefficients as the opportunity structure; the Box-Cox singles utility; the money-metric household welfare object; and the household welfare Gini as the baseline inequality measure. The first output is one table reporting the baseline welfare Gini, the welfare Gini after opportunity equalization, the welfare Gini after preference neutralization, the welfare Gini after both adjustments, and the corresponding Shapley shares, with a nested panel splitting the opportunity share into access and ability, and with the four pre-registered sensitivity scenarios reported alongside. The companion figure is one stacked bar showing the opportunity and preference components of household welfare inequality. The prototype is successful if it delivers a complete, reproducible estimation–welfare–decomposition pipeline on this sample and shows whether the opportunity component is empirically meaningful and stable under a narrow and transparent specification. All magnitudes remain placeholders until that prototype is accepted.
