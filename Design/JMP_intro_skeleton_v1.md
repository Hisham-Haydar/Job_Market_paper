## 1. Opening motivation paragraph

**Purpose:** Open the paper with the broad empirical and welfare problem, and make clear from the start that the paper is about the sources of welfare inequality rather than about a new policy simulation or a country ranking.

Structural labor-supply models are widely used to estimate preferences and evaluate policy, but they often treat labor supply as a choice from a common hours menu under a household-specific budget set. In many labor markets, however, households do not choose hours directly; they choose among jobs, and the set of feasible job opportunities differs across households. Once this is recognized, the interpretation of observed labor-market outcomes becomes less straightforward, because the same hours–income bundle may reflect unconstrained choice for one household and a constrained second-best for another. This paper starts from the concern that, if opportunity heterogeneity is left implicit, structural models will tend to absorb it into preference heterogeneity. That concern matters both for positive interpretation and for welfare analysis, and it motivates an empirical design in which opportunities are treated as a first-class object rather than as background structure. ## 2. Why opportunities matter paragraph

**Purpose:** Explain why the distinction between opportunities and preferences is economically substantive, not just a modeling refinement, and connect that point to the paper’s responsibility-sensitive interpretation.

A useful second paragraph should explain why unequal opportunities matter for welfare analysis, not only for labor-supply estimation. If two households reach similar observed outcomes but face different feasible job sets, then treating both outcomes as pure preference revelation is misleading. In that case, some of what looks like taste heterogeneity may instead reflect unequal access to work opportunities, hours packages, or wage-hours combinations. This is also where the paper can introduce its limited normative stance: unequal opportunities are interpreted as compensation-relevant circumstances, while preference heterogeneity is kept conceptually separate rather than being allowed to absorb constraints. The paragraph should stay empirical in tone and should not drift into the separate theory paper on axiomatic well-being measurement. ## 3. Core question paragraph

**Purpose:** State the main question sharply and make decomposition, rather than ranking, the central object of the paper.

The core question paragraph should state the paper’s main question in one clean sentence: how much of observed inequality in money-metric well-being is attributable to unequal job opportunities rather than to heterogeneous preferences, once labor supply is modeled as discrete choice among latent job packages and welfare is evaluated under constrained feasible sets. A second sentence should immediately add the sharper empirical interpretation: standard structural labor-supply models may overstate preference heterogeneity because they leave unequal job opportunities implicit. The paragraph should then say that the paper is not organized around country ranking, and not around a long list of policy simulations, but around the mechanism generating welfare inequality. That move is important because it tells the reader early that the paper’s contribution is explanatory and decompositional rather than descriptive. A final sentence can signal that the empirical object is the decomposition of household welfare inequality into opportunity and preference components. ## 4. Literature gap paragraph

**Purpose:** Sharpen the literature gap by identifying the three relevant neighboring literatures and the precise missing combination that the paper supplies.

This paragraph should be very explicit that the gap is not “nobody has studied opportunities” and not “nobody has studied welfare.” The first neighboring literature is constrained structural labor supply, including RURO and latent-jobs approaches, which already show that jobs should be modeled as packages and that opportunity densities matter for behavior, but usually treat welfare evaluation as an endpoint rather than making decomposition central. The second neighboring literature is empirical welfare analysis under heterogeneous preferences, which shows that money-metric welfare comparisons are sensitive to the treatment of preferences, but typically does not model latent job opportunities explicitly. The third neighboring literature is inequality-of-opportunity and responsibility-sensitive measurement, which provides the language of circumstances and compensation, but usually does not rest on a structural labor-supply model with estimated opportunities and tax-benefit-consistent budget mappings. The sharp gap is therefore the absence of a single empirical pipeline that estimates opportunities, carries them into welfare measurement, and then decomposes welfare inequality in an order-independent way. ## 5. Contribution paragraph

**Purpose:** State the paper’s contribution as a sequence of linked steps and make clear that the novelty is decomposition, not merely another structural model or another welfare metric.

The contribution paragraph should be written as a three-step logic. First, the paper estimates a structural labor-supply model with constrained opportunities and latent job packages rather than a model that treats all heterogeneity as preferences. Second, it constructs a household AEI-style money-metric welfare object defined relative to constrained feasible sets rather than to a universal choice set. Third, it decomposes the household welfare Gini into an opportunity component and a preference component using a two-factor Shapley-Shorrocks decomposition. A useful closing sentence is that the novelty is not “one more structural labor-supply model” and not “one more welfare-ranking exercise,” but the integration of opportunities, welfare, and decomposition into one empirical design. That sentence should also keep the paper distinct from the separate pure theory paper by emphasizing that the present project is an empirical structural JMP. ## 6. France empirical design paragraph

**Purpose:** Make the France grounding unmistakable and signal that the first empirical prototype is real, narrow, and feasible.

This paragraph should say clearly that the baseline empirical application is a **France 2016** structural labor-supply model based on **SRCV / EUROMOD-input microdata**. The implemented baseline is a **household/couples sample**, and the paper’s first decomposition is defined over household welfare levels rather than individual wage-hours outcomes. At the behavioral level, each spouse faces sampled latent job packages defined at the basic `job` level as wage-vigintile × 4-hour-interval opportunities plus a non-work option, while household disposable income for each sampled alternative is computed through EUROMOD. The welfare object is household AEI-style money-metric welfare relative to a joint non-work reference state, and the baseline inequality object is the Gini of household welfare. The paragraph should also acknowledge, briefly and without dramatizing it, that the France estimation is implemented but not yet fully stabilized, so the first version of the paper is a disciplined and deliberately narrow prototype rather than a claim of fully settled magnitudes. ## 7. Roadmap paragraph

**Purpose:** Tell the reader exactly how the rest of the paper is organized and reinforce the centrality of decomposition.

A simple roadmap paragraph can start by saying that the next section presents the structural framework and clarifies how preferences and opportunities enter the latent-jobs choice problem. The following section can describe the France 2016 couples sample, the sampled job representation, and the estimation strategy based on RURO with tax-benefit-consistent disposable income. A subsequent section can define the household AEI-style welfare object and explain why welfare is evaluated relative to constrained feasible sets rather than to a universal choice set. The next section can present the decomposition strategy, focusing on the household welfare Gini and the two-factor Shapley-Shorrocks decomposition based on opportunity equalization and preference neutralization. A final sentence can say that the empirical results section begins with one main table and one main figure designed to show whether the opportunity component of welfare inequality is empirically meaningful under the baseline France prototype, with robustness exercises postponed until after that baseline result is established.
