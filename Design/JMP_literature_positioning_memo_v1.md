## 1. Current state of the literature

The literature is now mature on each ingredient separately, but not on their integration. There is a strong structural labor-supply tradition that models jobs as constrained packages rather than as free hours choices on a common budget set. There is also a serious welfare literature showing that money-metric comparisons are sensitive to how preference heterogeneity is treated. And there is a normative literature on circumstances, responsibility, and compensation. What is still missing is a single empirical design that estimates latent opportunities, carries them into welfare measurement, and then decomposes welfare inequality in an order-independent way. That is exactly where your France 2016 couples prototype sits.  

The key point is that the field is no longer missing structural models, and it is no longer missing welfare metrics. What it is missing is a disciplined empirical pipeline in which opportunity heterogeneity is not just part of the econometric background but becomes the object of welfare decomposition. That is why decomposition, not another ranking or another reform table, is the right organizing principle for the JMP.  

## 2. Closest neighboring literatures

The first neighboring literature is **constrained structural labor supply / RURO / latent jobs**. This literature is directly relevant because it already models labor supply as choice over job packages with heterogeneous opportunity densities and warns that choice-set representation matters for counterfactuals. Your prototype belongs here on the positive side.  

The second neighboring literature is **empirical welfare analysis under heterogeneous preferences**. This literature establishes that welfare comparisons are normatively sensitive to the treatment of preferences, but it typically does not model opportunities explicitly. It is therefore close to your welfare layer, but incomplete relative to your question.  

The third neighboring literature is **inequality of opportunity / responsibility-sensitive measurement**. This literature provides the normative vocabulary of circumstances, compensation, and responsibility, but it usually does not rest on a structural labor-supply model with estimated latent opportunities and tax-benefit-consistent disposable income. It is close to your interpretation, but not to your empirical machinery.  

The fourth neighboring literature is **decomposition methodology**, especially Shapley-Shorrocks style decomposition for nonlinear objects. This is the least developed part of the substantive literature around your exact question, and therefore the part with the clearest room for contribution.  

## 3. Closest papers

The closest structural papers are **Aaberge, Dagsvik and Strøm (1995)**, **Aaberge, Colombino and Strøm (1999, 2000, 2004)**, **Aaberge and Colombino (2013, 2018)**, and **Dagsvik and Jia (2016)**. These are the papers that most clearly treat labor supply as choice among constrained job packages or latent jobs and connect that structure to welfare or policy counterfactuals. They are the closest antecedents to your structural contribution.  

The closest welfare paper is **Bargain et al. (2013)** on welfare, labor supply, and heterogeneous preferences. It is close because it shows that welfare rankings move once preference heterogeneity is taken seriously. It is not close on the opportunity side, because it does not model opportunities as a first-class object. 

The closest identification paper on the preferences-versus-opportunities margin is **Beffy et al. (2019)**, together with the broader restricted-choice and offer-distribution tradition. These papers are close because they show exactly why observed labor-supply outcomes cannot be read as pure preference revelation.  

The closest decomposition reference is **Audoly et al. (2025)** together with **Shorrocks (2013)** as the conceptual anchor. These are not substantive labor-supply papers, but they are closest to your decomposition contribution because they provide the order-independent rule your project needs.  

Finally, the closest normative bridge is the recent responsibility-sensitive measurement strand highlighted in your roadmap, because it shows how a structural job-choice model can be connected to a responsibility interpretation without turning the paper into a theory paper. 

## 4. What they do not yet do

The closest structural papers do **not** make welfare inequality decomposition the headline object. They estimate constrained labor supply and often compute welfare effects, but opportunity structure usually remains part of the behavioral architecture rather than becoming a measurable source of inequality in its own right. That is the main structural gap your project exploits.  

The closest welfare papers do **not** model job opportunities explicitly. They show that preference heterogeneity matters for welfare comparisons, but they leave open the possibility that some estimated “taste heterogeneity” is actually mismeasured opportunity heterogeneity. That is the main welfare gap your project exploits.  

The closest decomposition and inequality-of-opportunity papers do **not** rest on an estimated RURO-style labor-supply model with tax-benefit-consistent disposable income and household welfare measurement. They provide either decomposition tools or normative language, but not the full empirical pipeline. That is the main decomposition gap your project exploits.  

So the missing object in the literature is not another paper on constrained labor supply, not another paper on welfare metrics, and not another paper on circumstances. It is a **single empirical pipeline** that takes estimated latent opportunities seriously all the way from behavior to welfare to decomposition. 

## 5. My expected contribution

**Structural contribution:** estimate a France 2016 couples RURO / latent-jobs model in which job opportunities are modeled explicitly rather than absorbed into residual taste heterogeneity. The structural contribution is not that the model exists in the abstract, but that the opportunity mechanism is carried into the main empirical object instead of being treated as a nuisance term. 

**Welfare contribution:** construct household AEI-style money-metric welfare conditional on constrained feasible sets rather than on a universal choice set. This matters because it keeps welfare distinct from estimated behavioral utility and makes the interpretation of constrained outcomes coherent. 

**Decomposition contribution:** apply a two-factor Shapley-Shorrocks decomposition to the household welfare Gini so that opportunity heterogeneity and preference heterogeneity are attributed in an order-independent way. This is the central contribution of the paper. It is the difference between a paper about levels and a paper about mechanisms.  

**Empirical prototype contribution:** deliver the first decomposition result on the actual France 2016 couples baseline rather than on a stylized fallback sample. That is a real contribution because it anchors the project in an implemented, tax-benefit-consistent, household-level structural setting and creates a clean platform for later robustness to 2021, singles, and Germany.  

## 6. Why this project is scientifically meaningful

This is not just another welfare-ranking paper because the paper is not asking “who ranks where under metric (W).” It is asking what **drives** welfare inequality once opportunities are modeled explicitly. Rankings are endpoints; decomposition is a mechanism. That difference is substantive, not stylistic.  

This is not just another structural labor-supply paper because the paper does not stop at estimating behavior or simulating a reform. It uses the structural model to recover an opportunity mechanism, carries that mechanism into welfare evaluation, and then turns it into a decomposition of welfare inequality. The model is therefore not the end product; it is the engine of a new empirical object.  

The opportunity/decomposition angle matters scientifically because without it the field cannot tell whether observed welfare inequality reflects genuine preference heterogeneity or constrained access to jobs. If those are conflated, both welfare interpretation and policy conclusions become unstable. In that sense, the project is not merely adding one more variable to an existing setup; it is correcting a category error that sits at the intersection of identification and welfare analysis.  

## 7. Main vulnerability

The first likely objection is: **“You are claiming too much separation between preferences and opportunities.”** This is the core econometric objection, and it is serious. Your answer is not that the problem disappears, but that the paper is organized precisely around making that separation explicit, narrow, and empirically disciplined in the France 2016 couples baseline.  

The second likely objection is: **“This is still a normative exercise dressed up as empirics.”** That objection will come because AEI, responsibility, and compensation are normative choices. The answer is that the paper is explicit about its normative stance and keeps the normative layer narrow: opportunities are treated as compensation-relevant, preferences are separated as far as the structural model allows, and the core empirical output is a transparent decomposition rather than a broad ethical theory.  

The third likely objection is: **“Why should I care about France 2016 couples?”** This is the external-validity objection. The answer is that the paper’s first goal is not universal coverage but a credible proof of concept in a fully implemented baseline. The France 2016 couples prototype is the narrowest scope on which the structural model, welfare object, and decomposition rule already align. Later extensions are planned, but only after the first result exists.  

## 8. One-paragraph contribution statement

This paper makes a joint structural, welfare, decomposition, and empirical contribution. Structurally, it estimates labor supply in a France 2016 couples RURO / latent-jobs framework in which opportunities are modeled explicitly rather than absorbed into residual tastes. On the welfare side, it constructs household AEI-style money-metric welfare conditional on constrained feasible sets. On the decomposition side, it uses a two-factor Shapley-Shorrocks rule to decompose the household welfare Gini into an opportunity component and a preference component in an order-independent way. Empirically, it delivers this contribution in the narrowest fully coherent prototype currently available—France 2016 couples—so the paper’s contribution is not another welfare ranking and not another structural estimation exercise, but a first empirical demonstration of how unequal job opportunities can be measured as a distinct driver of welfare inequality.
